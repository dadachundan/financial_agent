## 🚀 Theme A — SpaceX & the SPCX IPO (the marquee event)

The S-1 dropped **2026-05-20**; Nasdaq dual-listing ticker **"SPCX"**, raise $50–75B, valuation **$1.75–2T** — the largest IPO ever. Three-segment story: **Space** (reusable launch, Starship V3 100t+, first flight May 2026), **Connectivity** (Starlink = the *only* profitable arm — 2025 rev $11.39B, EBITDA $7.2B / 63% margin, ~10.3M subs by Q1'26), **AI** (xAI merger, Colossus I/II ~1GW, orbital DCs, claimed $28.5T TAM). If you read one thing, read a *profile* — not the 201-page prospectus.

## 🛰️ Theme B — Orbital datacenters / space-based compute (AI × space — the hot, contested idea)

Worth your attention because the brokers *disagree*. **Bull case** (JPM "perpetual noon", the 太空算力 notes): LEO gives 24/7 solar + passive vacuum cooling, dissolving the power-and-heat ceiling throttling ground AI DCs; SpaceX talks ~100GW/yr orbital compute by ~2028, and Google / Starcloud-1 / Kepler / China's 国星宇航 ("星算计划") have *already flown* test compute sats — past sci-fi, into technical validation. **Skeptic case** (UBS SemiBytes, the essential counterweight): a 120–130kW rack needs ~⅓ of a football field of solar; launching a ~1.5t Blackwell rack costs ~$3M ≈ the rack's own price; orbital radiation shortens HBM life and flips bits; autonomous robotic assembly + polar-clustered orbits are unsolved. Honest read: real power/cooling logic, but the launch-mass and rad-hardening math doesn't close yet.
