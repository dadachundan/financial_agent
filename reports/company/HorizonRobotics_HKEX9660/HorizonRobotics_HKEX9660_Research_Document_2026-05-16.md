## 1. COMPANY OVERVIEW

Horizon Robotics (地平线机器人-W, HKEX:9660) is the leading Chinese provider of integrated software-and-hardware solutions for passenger-vehicle advanced driver assistance (ADAS) and higher-tier autonomous driving (AD). The company designs its own Journey (征程) family of automotive-grade AI System-on-Chips, builds production-stack software (Horizon Mono for basic ADAS, Horizon Pilot for highway NOA, and Horizon SuperDrive / HSD for full-scene urban autonomy), licenses algorithm and IP modules to OEMs, and provides one-stop development services so that automakers and Tier-1 suppliers can ship intelligent-driving features faster. By management's own framing, Horizon is the "Mobileye + Nvidia of China," fusing a vertically integrated chip-plus-software stack with an open-licensing business model ([Horizon Robotics 2024 年報, p. 5](https://www.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2025/0421/2025042100203.pdf)).

The company makes money through two automotive revenue lines and one small non-automotive line:

- **Product solutions (產品解決方案)** — sales of Journey processing hardware bundled with reference software stacks. Revenue rose 31.2% to RMB 664.2 m in FY2024 and then jumped 250% YoY to RMB 777.8 m in the first half of 2025 as the new Journey 6 family ramped ([2024 年報, p. 8](https://www.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2025/0421/2025042100203.pdf); [2025 中期報告, p. 9](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2025/0929/2025092901157.pdf)).
- **Licensing and services (授權及服務業務)** — IP licensing of algorithms, BPU IP blocks and software stacks, plus paid co-development with OEMs and Tier-1s. Revenue grew 70.9% to RMB 1,647 m in FY2024 (69.1% of total revenue) and added another 6.9% in H1-2025 to RMB 738 m ([2024 年報, p. 8](https://www.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2025/0421/2025042100203.pdf)).
- **Non-automotive (非車解決方案)** — robotics and AIoT chips through majority-owned subsidiary D-Robotics, contributing only RMB 71.9 m in FY2024 (3.0% of revenue) but growing in unit volume ([2024 年報, p. 8](https://www.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2025/0421/2025042100203.pdf)).

Total FY2024 revenue reached RMB 2,383.6 m (US$330 m), up 53.6% YoY, with a blended gross margin of 77.3% (up from 70.5% in 2023). H1-2025 revenue accelerated to RMB 1,566.8 m, up 67.6% YoY, although blended gross margin compressed to 65.4% as lower-margin product solutions took share from very-high-margin licensing ([2024 年報, p. 4](https://www.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2025/0421/2025042100203.pdf); [2025 中期報告, p. 4](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2025/0929/2025092901157.pdf)). Cumulative Journey shipments crossed 10 million units in August 2025 — the first Chinese intelligent-driving company to do so — and the company reports more than 400 design wins across more than 27 OEMs and 42 brands by mid-2025 ([2025 中期報告, p. 5](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2025/0929/2025092901157.pdf)). Operations are headquartered in Beijing's Haidian district with a major secondary base in Shanghai's Lin-gang free-trade zone, plus offices in Munich, Tokyo, Silicon Valley and Hong Kong; year-end 2024 headcount was 2,078 full-time employees ([2024 年報, p. 11](https://www.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2025/0421/2025042100203.pdf)).

**Valuation snapshot (as of 2026-05-15).** Horizon Robotics trades at HK$7.64, giving a market capitalization of roughly HK$102.9 bn (~US$13.2 bn) ([Yahoo Finance — 9660.HK](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/9660.HK/); [Bloomberg — 9660:HK](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/9660:HK)). On a TTM basis, revenue is approximately RMB 4.1 bn (combining FY2025 RMB 3,758 m released in March 2026 with the trailing four quarters; FY2025 reported revenue grew 57.7% YoY) ([Horizon Robotics FY2025 results, 19 Mar 2026](https://iis.aastocks.com/20260319/12059245-0.PDF)).

- **TTM P/S ≈ 28×** at the current price — extreme on any absolute scale, far above the auto-chip peer median.
- **TTM P/E is deeply negative.** FY2025 reported a net loss of RMB 10.47 bn — driven, paradoxically, by a non-cash fair-value loss on the CARIAD convertible loan and on certain other financial liabilities as the share price rose (the same mechanism that *created* a RMB 2.35 bn accounting profit in FY2024 when the conversion price fell) ([Horizon Robotics FY2025 results, 19 Mar 2026](https://iis.aastocks.com/20260319/12059245-0.PDF); [2024 年報, p. 10](https://www.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2025/0421/2025042100203.pdf)). Stripped of mark-to-market, share-based comp and IPO expenses, the **adjusted operating loss was RMB 1.50 bn in FY2024 and RMB 1.11 bn in H1-2025** — a real, cash-consuming loss reflecting R&D spend of RMB 3.16 bn in 2024 and RMB 2.30 bn in H1-2025 alone ([2024 年報, p. 7](https://www.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2025/0421/2025042100203.pdf); [2025 中期報告, p. 8](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2025/0929/2025092901157.pdf)). The negative P/E is therefore a hybrid: real cash-burn at the operating line (cash-burning growth) plus a large, optical non-cash charge (mark-to-market of pre-IPO convertibles) — both must be flagged.
- **Multiple range since IPO (Oct-2024 to May-2026):** the IPO priced at HK$3.99 with a first-day close of HK$4.10 (P/S roughly 15× on FY2024 revenue) and rallied past HK$10 in late-2025/early-2026 on Journey-6 mass-production catalysts (peak P/S ~36×) before settling near HK$7.60 in May-2026 ([SCMP, 24 Oct 2024](https://www.scmp.com/business/banking-finance/article/3283603/horizon-robotics-soar-31-hong-kong-debut-ipos-return-bumper-week-fundraising); [Yahoo Finance — 9660.HK history](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/9660.HK/history/)).
- **Peer comparison.** Mobileye (NASDAQ:MBLY) trades at P/S 3.4× with TTM P/E negative on a similar revenue base; market cap is roughly US$8.4 bn on US$2 bn of 2026 revenue ([Yahoo Finance — MBLY](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/MBLY/); [StockAnalysis — MBLY](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/mbly/)). Black Sesame Intelligence (HKEX:2533) — the closest pure-play Chinese ADAS-chip comp — trades at HK$18.93 with a market cap of HK$12.3 bn on FY2024 revenue of RMB 474 m, giving P/S ~22×, also TTM-loss-making ([Yahoo Finance — 2533.HK](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/2533.HK/)). Hesai (NASDAQ:HSAI) — LiDAR rather than chips, but the closest Chinese AD-stack listed comp — trades at roughly P/S 8× with positive EBITDA momentum. Qualcomm (NASDAQ:QCOM), the diversified scale player, trades at P/S 4–5× and P/E ~17×; its automotive business is only ~10% of revenue. Nvidia (NASDAQ:NVDA), although a more relevant computing-platform comp via its DRIVE Thor platform, is so dominated by data-center AI that its multiples (P/S ~22×, P/E ~40×) are not a like-for-like.
- **Interpretation.** A ~28× TTM P/S is high even for a Chinese ADAS-chip company growing 50–60% on revenue and gaining share. The premium reflects three narratives: (i) Horizon is the listed pure-play for the "intelligent driving equalization" (智駕平權) theme — basic ADAS becoming standard equipment on RMB 80k–150k vehicles; (ii) Journey 6 cumulative shipments crossed 10 million in 2025 with a market share of ~46% in basic ADAS and ~32% in mid-/high-end among Chinese OEMs ([2025 中期報告, p. 5](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2025/0929/2025092901157.pdf)); (iii) the Volkswagen / CARIZON tie-up offers a credible path to non-China revenue. The multiple compresses sharply if any one of these narratives breaks — see Section 9 valuation-risk.

## 2. COMPANY HISTORY

Horizon Robotics was founded in **Beijing on 21 July 2015** by Dr. Kai Yu (余凱), then vice-president of Baidu Research and the architect of one of China's earliest autonomous-driving R&D programmes. Dr. Yu was joined at incorporation by Dr. Chang Huang (黃暢), a computer-vision researcher who had been chief architect of Baidu's deep-learning institute, and Feifei Tao (陶斐雯), who ran operations at Baidu's USA office and would become Horizon's COO ([2024 年報, p. 39](https://www.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2025/0421/2025042100203.pdf)). The founding thesis was a single, bold technology bet: instead of building only software on third-party silicon, Horizon would design its own purpose-built Brain Processing Unit (BPU) — a domain-specific accelerator for vision and BEV (bird's-eye-view) workloads — and pair it with an end-to-end stack so that AI inference could meet automotive safety, latency, and cost constraints. The thesis was contrarian in 2015: Chinese fabless startups had largely avoided automotive silicon because of long qualification cycles and Tier-1 lock-in.

The first generation of Journey silicon, **Journey 1 (征程1)**, taped out in 2017 — a 40nm, ~1 TOPS visual perception SoC aimed at proof-of-concept ADAS systems. **Journey 2 (征程2)**, in 2019, was Horizon's first design win at series production, shipping on the Changan UNI-T in 2020 and marking China's first locally-designed automotive AI SoC in mass production. **Journey 3 (征程3)** in 2020 brought 5 TOPS for entry-level L2 ADAS and became a workhorse for legacy Tier-1 integrations. The architectural inflection arrived with **Journey 5 (征程5)** in 2021 — a 16nm, 128 TOPS chip that established Horizon as a credible alternative to Mobileye's EyeQ5 and Nvidia's Xavier for L2+ to L3 highway autonomy, winning placements on Li Auto (理想), BYD (比亚迪), GAC Aion (廣汽埃安), and SAIC (上汽) models from 2022 onward ([Wikipedia — Horizon Robotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horizon_Robotics); [Caixin, 25 Apr 2024](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2024-04-25/byd-backed-horizon-robotics-unveils-next-generation-auto-chips-102190176.html)).

Strategically, Horizon kept Journey 4 (征程4) as a niche derivative and skipped to **Journey 6 (征程6)** in April 2024, branded as a "full-scenario" family scaling from a sub-10W 6E/6M for entry-level ADAS up to a 560-TOPS Journey 6P aimed at urban NOA and HSD. Mass production of Journey 6 began in February 2025; by mid-2025 it accounted for 49.5% of unit shipments in the half ([2025 中期報告, p. 5](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2025/0929/2025092901157.pdf); [Gasgoo, 25 Apr 2024](https://autonews.gasgoo.com/icv/70035951.html)).

A defining corporate event was the **Volkswagen / CARIAD partnership announced in October 2022 and finalized in November 2023.** Under the deal, the Volkswagen Group committed approximately €2.4 bn (US$2.6 bn), split between a strategic equity investment into Horizon Robotics and a Beijing-headquartered joint venture, **CARIZON (酷睿程)**, registered with RMB 6.8 bn (~US$940 m) of paid-in capital. Volkswagen / CARIAD holds 60% of CARIZON and Horizon holds 40%; CARIZON is therefore equity-accounted as an associate by Horizon, with Horizon's 40% share of the JV's losses recognized in "share of investments accounted for using the equity method." CARIZON, as agreed, became the exclusive ADAS / AD software-stack supplier for Volkswagen's China-built MEB and CMP-platform electric vehicles, with first SOP targeted for 2026 ([CARIAD press release](https://cariad.technology/de/en/news/stories/joint-venture-cariad-horizon-robotics.html); [Volkswagen Group press release](https://www.volkswagen-group.com/en/press-releases/volkswagen-to-strengthen-regional-development-competence-for-autonomous-driving-in-china-through-joint-venture-between-cariad-and-horizon-robotics-16562); [Freshfields, 2023](https://www.freshfields.com/en-gb/news/2023/12/freshfields-advises-volkswagen-group-on-2.4bn-investment-in-china-smart-vehicle-technology-joint-venture-with-horizon-robotics/); [CnEVPost, 8 Dec 2023](https://cnevpost.com/2023/12/08/jv-between-cariad-horizon-robotics-established/)). In FY2024 alone, the JV contributed roughly RMB 352 m in licensing-and-services revenue in H1-2024 — about 51% of that line in the half ([Bamboo Works](https://thebambooworks.com/horizons-lead-grows-in-smart-driving-race-with-new-product-rollouts/)) — while also driving the bulk of Horizon's RMB 557 m equity-accounted loss from associates in 2024 ([2024 年報, p. 10](https://www.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2025/0421/2025042100203.pdf)).

Horizon raised more than US$3 bn of pre-IPO capital across roughly a dozen rounds between 2015 and 2022, with named investors including SAIC, Great Wall Motors, BYD, CATL, Hillhouse, 5Y Capital, Sequoia China, Yunfeng Capital, Baillie Gifford, and Alibaba ([Crunchbase — Horizon Robotics](https://www.crunchbase.com/organization/horizon-robotics); [Reuters via Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/horizon-robotics-said-draw-alibaba-085610445.html)). The **Hong Kong IPO on 24 October 2024** raised HK$5.41 bn (US$696 m) at the top of the marketed range (HK$3.99) and was the largest tech listing in Hong Kong in 2024 ([SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/business/banking-finance/article/3283603/horizon-robotics-soar-31-hong-kong-debut-ipos-return-bumper-week-fundraising); [Cleary Gottlieb](https://www.clearygottlieb.com/news-and-insights/news-listing/horizon-robotics-hk5-4-billion-offering-and-primary-listing-in-hong-kong); [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-10-22/horizon-robotics-raises-696-million-in-hong-kong-ipo); [Horizon press release](https://en.horizon.auto/horizon-robotics-officially-lists-on-the-hong-kong-stock-exchange/)). The listing also formalized a **dual-class structure**: Class A "weighted-voting-rights" shares (held principally by Dr. Yu) carry 10 votes per share; Class B (publicly traded) shares carry one vote each ([2024 年報, p. 18](https://www.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2025/0421/2025042100203.pdf)).

In the **last 18 months**, three developments matter most. First, Horizon delivered Journey 6 mass production from February 2025 and crossed 10 million cumulative Journey shipments in August 2025 — the first Chinese ADAS-chip vendor to do so. Second, the **Horizon SuperDrive (HSD)** urban-NOA solution, designed in-house and built on Journey 6P, was scheduled for SOP with multiple OEM design wins in Q3 2025 ([2025 中期報告, p. 6](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2025/0929/2025092901157.pdf)). Third, in March 2025 Horizon recapitalized its **D-Robotics** subsidiary (the non-automotive robotics-SoC arm) with a US$36.6 m Series A2, retaining control — clearly preparing the unit for a separate fundraising track focused on humanoid and service-robot brain SoCs ([2024 年報, p. 37](https://www.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2025/0421/2025042100203.pdf)). The 2025 results, reported in March 2026, showed revenue of RMB 3,758 m (+57.7% YoY) and a 47.7% basic-ADAS share in China ([Horizon Robotics FY2025 results announcement, 19 Mar 2026](https://iis.aastocks.com/20260319/12059245-0.PDF)).

## 3. MANAGEMENT TEAM

**Dr. Kai Yu (余凱) — Founder, Chairman & Chief Executive Officer (age 48; joined 2015).** Dr. Yu is the technical and commercial spine of Horizon. He holds a Bachelor's (1998) and Master's (2000) in Electronic Engineering from Nanjing University and a Ph.D. in Computer Science from Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München (2004), and spent roughly 12 years in Germany and the United States in senior R&D roles, including Senior Research Scientist at Siemens Corporate Research and Head of Media Analytics at NEC Laboratories America, with a concurrent adjunct teaching role at Stanford ([2024 年報, p. 39](https://www.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2025/0421/2025042100203.pdf)). From April 2012 to June 2015 he served as Vice-President of Baidu Research and initiated Baidu's first autonomous-driving programme in 2013 — one of the earliest such efforts in China. He has more than 100 published papers with over 30,000 citations ([2024 年報, p. 39](https://www.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2025/0421/2025042100203.pdf)). At Horizon, he sets architectural direction, drives the Journey roadmap, and personally negotiates strategic OEM and Tier-1 partnerships (including the Volkswagen / CARIAD deal). Investors view him as a category-defining technical founder; the dual-class WVR structure concentrates voting control in his hands, which is governance-positive for execution but governance-negative for minority shareholders. His credibility is reinforced by the fact that Horizon's chip-plus-stack thesis — once contrarian — is now the consensus path for Chinese intelligent driving.

**Dr. Chang Huang (黃暢) — Co-Founder, Executive Director & Chief Technology Officer (age 44; joined 2015).** Dr. Huang oversees R&D and the Journey-BPU architecture. He holds a Ph.D. in Computer Science and Technology from Tsinghua University (2007), spent three years as a postdoctoral researcher at the University of Southern California, and worked as a research scientist at NEC Laboratories America (2010–2012) and Principal Architect at Baidu USA and Beijing (2012–2015) — overlapping with Dr. Yu at Baidu ([2024 年報, p. 40](https://www.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2025/0421/2025042100203.pdf)). He has more than 20,000 academic citations and over 80 international patents ([2024 年報, p. 40](https://www.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2025/0421/2025042100203.pdf)). Inside Horizon he runs the BPU IP team, the Journey hardware programme, and the algorithms organization (perception, planning, end-to-end models). Industry coverage credits Dr. Huang with the architectural decisions on Journey 5 and Journey 6 that allowed Horizon to scale BEV-transformer workloads at a power envelope competitive with Mobileye EyeQ and well below Nvidia Orin/Thor.

**Ms. Feifei Tao (陶斐雯) — Former Co-Founder, COO & Executive Director (resigned 27 August 2025) and Dr. Jian Xu (徐健) — incoming Executive Director from August 2025.** Ms. Tao co-founded Horizon and ran finance, HR, legal, marketing and capital-markets activities from inception, having previously held operations and sales roles at Baidu and Google ([2024 年報, p. 39](https://www.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2025/0421/2025042100203.pdf)). Her resignation immediately after the company's first full annual reporting cycle as a listed entity, in August 2025, drew analyst scrutiny — particularly given that the company simultaneously rotated a board member affiliated with SAIC ([2025 中期報告, p. 2](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2025/0929/2025092901157.pdf)). She was succeeded as Executive Director by Dr. Jian Xu (徐健), an internal appointment whose biography is provided in the 2025 interim report. Dr. Xu's appointment suggests an operating-rather-than-finance orientation as the company shifts from R&D-burn to production-scale execution.

**Dr. Liming Chen (陳黎明) — Executive Director & President (age 62; joined 2021; appointed Executive Director in March 2024).** Dr. Chen is the senior automotive-industry hire who anchors Horizon's credibility with OEMs and Tier-1s. He spent nearly 30 years at **Bosch (博世)**, rising to Senior Vice-President and Regional President of Bosch China's Chassis Control Systems business (2012–2021), where he ran the P&L for one of Bosch's largest non-Germany operating units and built Bosch's largest foreign-invested automotive R&D center in China ([2024 年報, p. 41](https://www.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2025/0421/2025042100203.pdf)). He led the engineering of Bosch's TCS traction-control system (still used in the current Bosch ESP10) and shipped multiple generations of chassis-control products globally. He holds a Bachelor's and Master's in aerospace power-plant control from Nanjing University of Aeronautics and Astronautics (1983, 1986) and a Ph.D. in Mechanical Engineering from Wayne State University (1995). At Horizon his remit is overall management with explicit accountability for supply chain and quality assurance — areas where Horizon has had to upgrade processes substantially as Journey shipments scaled past 1 million units per year. His Bosch pedigree is a useful counterweight to the Baidu / academic profile of the founding team.

**Board composition and governance.** The board has 12 directors: four executive, four non-executive, and four independent. The four non-executive directors include Mr. Liang Li (Hillhouse Partner), Mr. Qin Liu (Founding Partner of 5Y Capital), Dr. André Stoffels (CFO of CARIAD SE — Volkswagen's representative), and Mr. Jianjun Zhang (SAIC representative, appointed August 2025 replacing Dr. Juehui Zhang). The four independent directors include Prof. Jun Pu (accounting professor, Audit Committee chair), Mr. Yingqiu Wu (Nomination chair), Dr. Katherine Rong Xin, and Dr. Ya-Qin Zhang (formerly Microsoft / Baidu, ESG Committee chair) ([2024 年報, p. 38](https://www.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2025/0421/2025042100203.pdf)). The audit, remuneration, nomination and ESG committees are each chaired by an independent director. Insider control is concentrated through the **WVR structure** — Class A shares with 10× voting power are held principally by Dr. Yu, which under HKEX 8A.18 means the founder retains practical voting control even after the IPO float. This is a governance risk for minority shareholders (no recourse against a controlling founder); but Hong Kong's WVR sunset rules require automatic conversion if the holder ceases to be a director.

## 4. PRODUCTS & SERVICES

Horizon's portfolio is organized along three layers — **silicon (Journey + Sunrise SoCs)**, **software stacks (Mono / Pilot / SuperDrive)**, and **operating-system / tooling (Halo OS, OpenExplorer, AIDI)** — plus a separate non-automotive arm under D-Robotics. Every product line is documented at [en.horizon.auto](https://en.horizon.auto/) and the Chinese mainland site [horizon.cc](https://horizon.cc/).

**Silicon — the Journey (征程) automotive SoC family.** This is the flagship layer; FY2024 product-solutions revenue is essentially Journey chips bundled with reference stacks.

- **Journey 2 (征程2)** — 40nm, ~4 TOPS visual SoC for legacy L1/L2 ADAS. Launched 2019; the first Chinese automotive AI SoC in mass production. Long-tail product today. *Competitive verdict: partial moat (first-mover among Chinese chips, but technologically eclipsed)*. Closest competitor: Mobileye EyeQ4 (2018) — Mobileye ahead on shipping volume globally, Horizon ahead in China-specific OEM integrations.
- **Journey 3 (征程3)** — 16nm, 5 TOPS, single front-camera-class ADAS workloads. Launched 2020. Workhorse for the bottom of the ADAS market. *Competitive verdict: partial moat — cost-efficient, deeply integrated with low-end Tier-1 stacks*. Closest competitor: Mobileye EyeQ5 / TI TDA4VM.
- **Journey 5 (征程5)** — 16nm, 128 TOPS, BEV-capable. Launched 2021; first Chinese AI SoC qualified for L2+/L3 highway autonomy. Powers Li Auto L9, Li L8 Pro, BYD Han DM-i, GAC Aion LX, SAIC Roewe RX5, etc. *Competitive verdict: yes — moat from in-house BPU IP (cost-and-power-optimized for BEV/transformer workloads), large installed base of OEM software co-developed against it (switching cost), and qualified supply chain*. Closest competitor: Nvidia Orin-N / Mobileye EyeQ6H — Orin offers more raw TOPS at a higher power and price; EyeQ6H offers higher integration in a "black-box" model; Journey 5 is *at parity* on capability and *ahead* on China-OEM integration and price.
- **Journey 6 (征程6) family** — N7 or N5-class node, six SKUs (6B / 6L / 6E / 6M / 6H / 6P) spanning ~10 to 560 TOPS, launched April 2024 with mass production from February 2025. The 6E and 6M target high-volume basic-to-mid ADAS at low cost; the 6P (560 TOPS) targets full-scene urban NOA / HSD ([Caixin, 25 Apr 2024](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2024-04-25/byd-backed-horizon-robotics-unveils-next-generation-auto-chips-102190176.html); [Gasgoo, 25 Apr 2024](https://autonews.gasgoo.com/icv/70035951.html); [TP Huang Substack](https://tphuang.substack.com/p/horizon-robotics-unveiled-journey)). Initial customers at launch included SAIC, Volkswagen, BYD, Li Auto, GAC, Deepal, BAIC, Chery, Exeed Sterra, Voyah; Tier-1 partners included Bosch, Luxshare, Huaqin, TzTek ([Caixin](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2024-04-25/byd-backed-horizon-robotics-unveils-next-generation-auto-chips-102190176.html)). *Competitive verdict: yes — strongest in-house moat (proprietary "Nash" BPU architecture, scalable family that lets a single OEM cover its entire price range with one toolchain), accelerating design-win run-rate (>100 model wins in 2024 alone), and unit-price uplift driving 250% YoY product-solutions revenue in H1-2025*. Closest competitors: Nvidia DRIVE Thor (1,000 TOPS, higher cost/power), Qualcomm Snapdragon Ride (cabin+ADAS integration), Mobileye EyeQ6H/EyeQ Ultra — Horizon is *at parity to ahead* on cost-performance for China OEMs and *behind* Nvidia on absolute headroom for L4 robotaxi.

**Software stacks — Mono / Pilot / SuperDrive.**
- **Horizon Mono** — single-front-camera ADAS stack (AEB, LKA, ACC, lane-departure warning), targeting commodity-priced cars. Horizon held #1 share in China's basic-ADAS market by H1-2025 (45.8% among Chinese OEMs) ([2025 中期報告, p. 5](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2025/0929/2025092901157.pdf)). *Competitive verdict: yes — cost moat from in-house silicon plus algorithm tuning; channel moat with Bosch and Continental*. Closest competitor: Mobileye EyeQ-based vision-only ADAS — Mobileye ahead in Europe / NA, Horizon ahead in China.
- **Horizon Pilot** — multi-sensor highway-NOA stack on Journey 5/6 hardware. Underpins highway NOA on Li Auto, BYD God's Eye, GAC Aion, SAIC. *Competitive verdict: yes — large installed base creates data-network-effect for OTA improvement*. Closest competitor: Huawei ADS 2.0, Xpeng XNGP. Horizon *at parity* on highway NOA scenarios; *behind* Huawei on urban NOA without HSD.
- **Horizon SuperDrive (HSD)** — full-scene end-to-end urban-NOA stack built on Journey 6P, announced 2024, slated for SOP from Q3 2025 ([2024 年報, p. 5](https://www.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2025/0421/2025042100203.pdf); [2025 中期報告, p. 6](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2025/0929/2025092901157.pdf)). Won the iF Design Award 2025 for its HMI ([2024 年報, p. 6](https://www.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2025/0421/2025042100203.pdf)). *Competitive verdict: partial — technology is credible but commercial proof comes only with the H2-2025 SOPs*. Closest competitor: Huawei ADS 3.0, Xpeng XNGP, Tesla FSD (China). Horizon *behind* Huawei and Tesla on urban-NOA real-world miles today; the comparison reopens once HSD-equipped vehicles reach customers at scale.

**OS / tooling — Halo, OpenExplorer, AIDI.** Halo is Horizon's vehicle-domain operating system that hosts the Journey BPU drivers, ROS-style middleware, and safety primitives. OpenExplorer is the developer SDK / model-zoo for porting customer perception networks onto Journey hardware; AIDI is the closed-loop data-and-model factory that ingests fleet data and retrains models for OTA push. *Competitive verdict: partial — necessary scaffolding for the chip+stack pitch and a real switching cost for any OEM that has invested in Halo/OpenExplorer toolchain integration.* Closest competitor: Nvidia DRIVE OS / DriveWorks. Nvidia *ahead* on developer ecosystem worldwide; Horizon *ahead* on China-OEM proprietary requirements.

**Licensing IP — BPU, perception, planning IP blocks.** Horizon licenses BPU IP blocks and end-to-end perception IP to OEMs and Tier-1s that wish to build their own SoCs while still leaning on Horizon's algorithms. This is the highest-margin revenue line (92% gross margin in FY2024) ([2024 年報, p. 9](https://www.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2025/0421/2025042100203.pdf)) and is the main vehicle by which CARIZON, Volkswagen, Li Auto, BYD and others contract for co-developed software. *Competitive verdict: yes — quasi-monopoly position in China as the only credible licensable ADAS algorithm stack outside Huawei.* Closest competitor: Mobileye-licensed Roadbook / REM IP; Mobileye is unwilling to license at the depth Horizon will.

**Non-automotive — D-Robotics RDK X3 / X5, Sunrise SoCs.** D-Robotics is the 80%-plus-owned subsidiary that ships Sunrise X3 (4-core A53 + 5 TOPS BPU) and Sunrise X5 (8-core A55 + 10 TOPS BPU, with Wi-Fi 6 / BT5.4 / CAN-FD / PoE for robotics) chips and the matching RDK X3 / RDK X5 developer kits ([CNX Software — RDK X5](https://www.cnx-software.com/2025/06/30/d-robotics-rdk-x5-development-board-features-sunrise-x5-octa-core-soc-with-10-tops-bpu-for-ros-projects/); [D-Robotics product page](https://en.d-robotics.cc/rdkx3); [Waveshare — RDK X3](https://www.waveshare.com/rdk-x3.htm)). Target customers are humanoid integrators, service-robot makers and AIoT developers; pricing is open-market (US$80–500 retail at distributors). *Competitive verdict: partial — Sunrise BPU IP is genuinely differentiated for cost-constrained robotics applications, but the segment is still <5% of revenue.* Closest competitor: Nvidia Jetson Orin Nano / NX, Rockchip RK3588, Qualcomm RB5/RB6. Nvidia *ahead* on developer ecosystem; D-Robotics *competitive* on cost and ROS toolchain integration.

**Flagship vs. long-tail.** Three products drive the business today: (1) **Journey 6 family** (now ~50% of unit shipments and the primary growth engine — product-solutions revenue up 250% YoY in H1-2025 ([2025 中期報告, p. 9](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2025/0929/2025092901157.pdf))); (2) **Horizon Mono** software stack on Journey 6E/6M, which captures the basic-ADAS share; (3) **the IP-and-services licensing line**, which generates the bulk of gross profit (RMB 1,516 m gross profit in FY2024, 92% gross margin). Journey 2/3 are long-tail; HSD and D-Robotics products are pre-scale optionality.

**Last-12-month launches / sunsets.** Journey 6B was "lit up" (silicon validation) in August 2025, completing the J6 family; Journey 6E and 6M entered SOP in H1-2025; HSD released its v2 algorithm with end-to-end VLA-style behavior planning in early 2026; Sunrise X5 board (RDK X5) launched in 2025. No publicly announced sunsets.

## 6. INDUSTRY OVERVIEW

**Industry definition.** Horizon operates at the intersection of (a) automotive semiconductors (under NAICS 334413), (b) automotive software (NAICS 511210), and (c) the broader "intelligent driving" stack — defined locally as ADAS, NOA (Navigate on Autopilot), and AD (autonomous driving) for passenger vehicles. The adjacent industry is robotics SoC (humanoid and service robots), through D-Robotics.

**Market size and structure.** Global automotive AI / ADAS-and-AD compute platform revenue is on a steep growth path: industry trackers estimate the global autonomous-driving chips market at roughly US$5 bn in 2024, on track for ~US$25–35 bn by 2030 at 25–30% CAGR ([GMI Insights — Autonomous Driving Chips](https://www.gminsights.com/industry-analysis/autonomous-driving-chips-market)). China is the world's largest auto market by units (~28 m passenger vehicles in 2024) and now also the most ADAS-penetrated. According to Horizon's H1-2025 disclosure, **ADAS penetration in China rose from 51% at end-2024 to 59% in H1-2025**, and the mid-/high-end NOA subset rose from 20% to 32% of new intelligent-vehicle sales in the same period ([2025 中期報告, p. 5](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2025/0929/2025092901157.pdf)). Chinese self-brand OEMs now hold >63% of domestic passenger-vehicle sales — and they are the heaviest adopters of ADAS / NOA, providing the largest single SAM for Horizon.

**Growth rates.** Chinese self-brand share of domestic passenger vehicles grew from ~44% in 2021 to >63% in H1-2025; ADAS penetration rose from <15% in 2018 to ~59% in H1-2025. The supplier industry has compounded faster than the end-market because per-vehicle ADAS-content dollar values are rising — entry-level cars are now being equipped with Mono ADAS that 5 years ago would have been L0, and mid-trim cars are being equipped with highway NOA that 5 years ago was a luxury option.

**Key trends.**
1. **"Intelligent-driving equalization" (智駕平權)** — the industry-wide push, accelerated in 2025, to make basic ADAS and even highway NOA standard equipment on RMB 80,000–150,000 vehicles. Horizon's Journey 6B/6E/6M family is purpose-built for this trend.
2. **End-to-end and VLA (vision-language-action) models** — the rapid migration from modular perception-plan-control pipelines to monolithic learned-policy networks, which raises the bar on training compute, data infrastructure, and inference TOPS-per-watt at the edge.
3. **Vertical integration by OEMs** — Tesla, BYD, Li Auto, Nio and Xpeng have all stated intent to design or co-design AD compute in-house, but each remains a Horizon (or Nvidia / Qualcomm / Huawei) customer in some form because in-house silicon timelines are 3–5 years.
4. **L4 robotaxi re-acceleration** — the Waymo / Baidu Apollo / Pony.ai / WeRide commercial expansions in 2025 have pulled L4 compute requirements forward, with Horizon explicitly targeting "L2-to-L4 evolution" as a strategic priority ([2025 中期報告, p. 7](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2025/0929/2025092901157.pdf)).
5. **Export-control risk** — US restrictions on advanced lithography (under 14nm) for Chinese fabless customers, and on AI training silicon (H100, B200) for Chinese end-users, push Chinese ADAS companies to localize fabrication (SMIC N+1/N+2) or to optimize for older nodes.

**Regulatory environment.** China's intelligent-driving regulatory framework is shaped by MIIT (工信部) standards GB/T 40429 (driving-automation classifications, aligned with SAE J3016), the Cyberspace Administration's vehicle-data localization rules, and the 2024 MIIT pilot programme allowing conditional L3 testing on selected highways. Hong Kong listing rules (Chapter 18C) and the WVR provisions (Chapter 8A) shape Horizon's governance.

**Industry dynamics.** The Chinese ADAS-chip industry is consolidating around three tiers: (i) **Foreign incumbents** — Nvidia (DRIVE Orin/Thor), Mobileye (EyeQ), Qualcomm (Snapdragon Ride), Tesla FSD (captive); (ii) **Chinese pure-plays** — Horizon Robotics, Black Sesame Intelligence, Cambricon (limited automotive); (iii) **Vertically integrated Chinese OEM/internet** — Huawei (ADS / MDC), Xpeng (Turing), Nio (Shenji NX9031), BYD (Xuanji). Buyer power is meaningful (a handful of large OEMs are the demand backbone) but supplier power is also meaningful — TSMC and Samsung are the foundries; ASML is the litho monopoly. Switching costs at the *integration level* are high once an OEM has invested in a Horizon Halo/OpenExplorer toolchain for a platform programme; switching costs at the *new-platform level* are low because every OEM dual-sources or runs RFQs each cycle.

## 7. COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

**Direct competitors (silicon + stack).**

1. **Nvidia (NASDAQ:NVDA) — DRIVE Orin / DRIVE Thor.** Nvidia's Orin (~254 TOPS) is the workhorse high-end ADAS SoC globally, deployed on Li Auto, Nio, Xpeng, Polestar, BYD's top trims, Volvo, Mercedes-Benz, and many others. DRIVE Thor (up to 2,000 TOPS) is the next platform. Nvidia *ahead* on raw performance, on the developer ecosystem (CUDA, DriveWorks), and on global penetration; *at parity to behind* on TOPS-per-dollar for the Chinese mid-market that Horizon dominates. Horizon's competitive answer is the Journey 6P at 560 TOPS, lower price, qualified locally ([recodechinaai Substack](https://recodechinaai.substack.com/p/tesla-fsds-toughest-competition-comes); [drrobertcastellano Substack](https://drrobertcastellano.substack.com/p/nvidias-dominance-in-adas-processors)).

2. **Mobileye (NASDAQ:MBLY) — EyeQ family.** Mobileye is the global volume leader in vision-based ADAS, with EyeQ4/EyeQ5/EyeQ6 shipping at ~30 m units a year combined to BMW, GM, Audi, Ford, Toyota and others. Mobileye *ahead* on global OEM relationships and on REM crowd-mapping data; *behind* in China, where Mobileye lost share to Horizon and to OEM-captive stacks. Mobileye's "supervised" SuperVision (Zeekr) and forthcoming Chauffeur are the analog of Horizon Pilot / HSD. Mobileye trades at P/S 3.4× ([Yahoo Finance — MBLY](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/MBLY/)); Horizon trades at ~28× — the gap reflects Horizon's growth profile and China-narrative premium.

3. **Qualcomm (NASDAQ:QCOM) — Snapdragon Ride / SA8540 / SA8775.** Qualcomm's auto business hit a ~US$3 bn run-rate in 2025 and integrates cabin compute with ADAS, addressing OEMs that prefer one-vendor consolidation. Qualcomm *ahead* on cabin-plus-ADAS bundling and on cellular IP; *behind* on dedicated ADAS market share in China. Snapdragon Ride is a credible high-end competitor to Journey 6P on Geely-Zeekr and on select German OEMs.

4. **Black Sesame Intelligence (HKEX:2533) — Huashan-A (華山) and Wudang-W (武當) series.** The closest pure-play Chinese listed comp; A1000 (58 TOPS) and A2000 (250+ TOPS) target similar OEMs. Market cap roughly HK$12 bn on FY2024 revenue of RMB 474 m — TTM P/S ~22× ([Yahoo Finance — 2533.HK](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/2533.HK/)). Differentiates with multi-domain-controller architectures.

Huawei is *vertically integrated* (chip + stack + cloud + HiCar cabin), addressing select OEMs (Aito, Avita, Stelato, Luxeed). Huawei *ahead* on urban-NOA today; *constrained* by US export rules on the most advanced lithography. Horizon's positioning relative to Huawei is "open ecosystem vs. captive system" — Horizon's chip+stack is licensable; Huawei's typically is not, which appeals to OEMs that want IP independence.

6. **Tesla FSD (captive).** Not a merchant supplier, but its data scale and end-to-end model architecture set the technical bar; FSD-on-HW4 is the benchmark Horizon HSD is measured against.

7. **Vertically integrated Chinese OEMs.** **Xpeng Turing**, **Nio Shenji NX9031** (5nm, 1000+ TOPS), **BYD Xuanji**, and **Li Auto MindGPT-VLA** — internal SoC efforts that could in time displace Horizon at those specific OEMs. Mitigant: long timelines (3–5 years), IP-licensing requirements (BYD, SAIC and others license Horizon BPU IP for their own programs).

8. **Cambricon (SSE:688256)** — primarily data-center AI; limited automotive presence but a possible adjacent threat.

9. **Tier-1 incumbents (Bosch, Continental, Aptiv, Valeo, ZF).** Mostly integrator-customers of Horizon, but with their own ADAS software stacks and "domain ECU" reference designs.

10. **Indirect / emerging — Hesai (NASDAQ:HSAI) and RoboSense (HKEX:2498).** Not direct competitors but adjacent stack-providers (LiDAR) whose customer relationships and OEM trust create cross-selling friction.

**Market positioning framework.** On a price/feature/scale 3-axis frame, Horizon sits in a unique cell: **lower price than Nvidia Orin/Thor, higher integration than Mobileye EyeQ (because the stack is licensable), higher unit-volume scale in China than any merchant competitor, but lower global reach than Nvidia/Mobileye/Qualcomm**. Horizon's market shares as disclosed: 45.8% of Chinese-OEM basic-ADAS solutions and 32.4% of mid-/high-end NOA solutions in H1-2025 ([2025 中期報告, p. 5](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2025/0929/2025092901157.pdf)).

**Advantages.** (1) Proprietary BPU architecture optimized for BEV / transformer workloads at automotive power envelopes; (2) Largest Chinese ADAS installed base = data network effects for stack improvement; (3) Open licensing model addresses OEMs who refuse Huawei's captive model; (4) Vertically integrated chip-plus-stack-plus-tooling = cost and reliability moat; (5) Volkswagen / CARIZON anchor for international expansion. **Vulnerabilities.** (1) Customer concentration with top-5 at 71.8%; (2) Customer-as-competitor risk from in-housing OEMs; (3) Dependence on TSMC / Samsung for advanced nodes (geopolitical export-control risk); (4) Capital intensity — RMB 3.16 bn of R&D in 2024 alone funded by IPO proceeds, with adjusted operating loss of RMB 1.5 bn FY2024 and RMB 1.1 bn H1-2025; (5) Catch-up risk versus Huawei on urban NOA.

## 8. MARKET OPPORTUNITY (TAM)

**TAM.** A reasonable bottoms-up TAM for Horizon spans three layers: (i) global automotive AI compute SoC market; (ii) automotive intelligent-driving software-and-services market; (iii) robotics SoC market.

- **Automotive AI SoC TAM.** Roughly US$5 bn in 2024 → US$25–35 bn by 2030 at ~25–30% CAGR ([GMI Insights](https://www.gminsights.com/industry-analysis/autonomous-driving-chips-market)). China is the largest market by units (28 m+ PV sales × ~60–80% ADAS penetration by 2030 at $50–150 per-vehicle silicon ASP).
- **ADAS / AD software-and-services TAM.** Bottoms-up: 28 m Chinese PV/year × ~50% NOA penetration by 2028 × ~US$300–700 per-vehicle software licensing value = US$4–10 bn/yr in China alone by 2028, plus a similar-or-larger global figure dominated by Mobileye / Nvidia today.
- **Robotics SoC TAM.** Earlier-stage and far smaller today, but the humanoid bull case is real: BofA, Goldman and Morgan Stanley have all published 2030 humanoid TAM estimates ranging US$24–38 bn for robot hardware, with edge-AI silicon a 5–10% slice, implying ~US$1–4 bn humanoid-SoC TAM by 2030.

**SAM (serviceable).** Horizon's SAM is principally Chinese OEMs plus joint-venture OEMs operating in China, plus the subset of global OEMs willing to qualify a Chinese supplier. By unit-volume math: Chinese passenger vehicles ~28 m/yr × ~70% addressable by Horizon (i.e., excluding Tesla, captive-Huawei OEMs, and pure-foreign-stack OEMs) × ~US$80–150 SoC ASP for Journey 6 family = US$1.5–3.0 bn/yr addressable revenue in China by 2028. Adding licensing-and-services on top (typically ~2× the SoC SAM, given Horizon's mix in 2024), the total Horizon SAM in 2028 is plausibly US$5–8 bn — vs. FY2025 revenue of ~US$550 m today, implying ample headroom even on a conservative market-share path.

**SOM (capturable share).** Horizon's H1-2025 China-OEM ADAS-share (~46% basic, ~32% mid-/high-end) is unlikely to be replicated globally because of geopolitical export-control restrictions and OEM relationships. But within China, holding 30–40% share is realistic. Applying 30–35% share to the SAM range above gives US$1.5–2.8 bn of FY2028 China-only revenue (3–6× current FY2025 revenue). International revenue (CARIZON + Japan OEMs + others) could plausibly add ~US$0.5–1.0 bn by 2028 if the Volkswagen SOPs and Japanese expansion deliver. That is broadly consistent with sell-side base-case FY2028 revenue estimates of US$2–3 bn.

**Penetration strategy.** Three vectors. **First**, drive Journey 6E/6M into the basic-ADAS standardization wave to capture entry-level units — that is the volume base, and it converts every Chinese sub-RMB 150k vehicle into a Horizon-attach. **Second**, drive Journey 6P + HSD into the mid-/high-end urban-NOA wave at OEM platform programmes — that is the dollar-content uplift (per-vehicle revenue may quintuple vs. basic ADAS). **Third**, internationalize through CARIZON (VW platform programmes from 2026 SOP) and through the two named Japanese OEM design wins (~7.5 m lifetime units committed). **Fourth (optionality)**, scale D-Robotics into the humanoid / service-robot SoC market — a small contribution today but a potential second-act if the humanoid market reaches the volumes Tesla / Unitree / Figure / Agility / XPeng / Xiaomi forecasters expect.

## 9. RISK ASSESSMENT

### Company-Specific Risks

**Customer concentration (material).** Top-5 customers = **71.8% of FY2024 revenue**; largest single customer = **31.5%**; top-1 was 37.6% in H1-2024 and top-5 was 77.9% per the IPO prospectus ([2024 年報, p. 19](https://www.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2025/0421/2025042100203.pdf); [36Kr](https://eu.36kr.com/en/p/3003347645980422)). Both metrics exceed the company-research thresholds (top-1 > 20%, top-5 > 50%) and qualify as **material risk**. If CARIZON-Volkswagen, Li Auto, or BYD reduce engagement (in-house SoC ramp, vendor diversification, or program cancellation), revenue could decline meaningfully on any one customer's withdrawal. Mitigant: the licensing-and-services contracts are multi-year master agreements with platform-programme commitments; design-win pipeline (>400 models by H1-2025) is diversifying.

**Customer-as-competitor / vertical-integration risk.** Multiple top customers (BYD Xuanji, Li Auto MindGPT, SAIC Zhiji, Xpeng Turing in adjacent OEM relationships) are building or licensing in-house ADAS silicon and stacks. If any of these efforts succeed at the silicon level, Horizon could be displaced over the next 3–5 years. Mitigant: Horizon's IP-licensing model captures revenue even when OEMs design their own SoCs (since they often license Horizon BPU and algorithm IP).

**Key-person dependency.** Dr. Kai Yu's WVR voting control and personal brand are tightly tied to Horizon's strategic credibility. The August 2025 resignation of co-founder COO Feifei Tao is a governance signal that warrants monitoring ([2025 中期報告, p. 2](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2025/0929/2025092901157.pdf)). Loss of any of Yu / Huang / Chen would damage execution. Mitigant: an experienced bench (Bosch / Audi / SAIC pedigrees on the board and at VP layer); WVR sunset rules ensure conversion to one-share-one-vote if Dr. Yu departs.

**Execution risk — HSD and Journey 6P.** Horizon SuperDrive is the company's biggest near-term technical bet. If HSD does not ship at the H2-2025 to H1-2026 promised cadence with quality competitive with Huawei ADS 4.0 or Tesla FSD-in-China, the urban-NOA share story stalls. R&D burn of RMB 2.3 bn in H1-2025 alone implies that execution slippage would directly compress the multiple. Mitigant: Journey 6 design wins (>100 in 2024 and >100 in H1-2025) and HSD-SOP timeline endorsed by multiple OEMs.

**Supplier concentration and node access.** Horizon relies on a small number of foundries (TSMC for Journey 5/6 base, Samsung for select SKUs) and on a small number of mask / IP suppliers. Top-5 suppliers represented 37.8% of 2024 procurement; top-1 was 10.2% ([2024 年報, p. 19](https://www.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2025/0421/2025042100203.pdf)). US export controls on advanced lithography to Chinese fabless customers create a tail risk for Journey 6P / 7 nodes.

**Related-party governance — CARIZON.** CARIZON is both a 40%-owned associate and a top customer; the CARIAD convertible loan (US$800 m original) sits on Horizon's balance sheet and drives multi-billion RMB swings in reported net income via fair-value remeasurement of the conversion option ([2024 年報, p. 19](https://www.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2025/0421/2025042100203.pdf)). The HKEX connected-transactions framework requires audit-committee review of these flows; the optics for minority shareholders are nonetheless complicated.

### Industry / Market Risks

**Competitive intensity from Nvidia, Huawei, and OEM-captive stacks.** Nvidia DRIVE Thor and Huawei ADS 4.0 set continuing pressure on Horizon's high-end positioning; OEM-captive stacks erode the merchant ADAS market over time. Mitigant: open-licensing model and superior cost-performance for Chinese mass-market.

**Geopolitical export controls and Chinese-stack restriction.** Tightening US restrictions on advanced-node access for Chinese fabless companies and on AI-training silicon for Chinese cloud customers could constrain Horizon's roadmap (Journey 7, future training infrastructure). Conversely, China's "intelligent-driving substitution" policy supports the local-supplier thesis.

**Regulatory risk on AD liability and data localization.** Evolving regulations on L3 / L4 liability allocation, on vehicle-data export, and on AI-training data could increase compliance cost and restrict cross-border product launches.

**End-market cyclicality and intelligent-driving plateau.** If Chinese PV sales decelerate or ADAS penetration plateaus below current trajectory, Horizon's growth multiple compresses sharply.

### Financial Risks

**Profitability timeline / persistent operating loss.** Adjusted operating loss was RMB 1.50 bn FY2024 and RMB 1.11 bn H1-2025; reported net loss was RMB 10.47 bn in FY2025 (largely non-cash) ([2024 年報, p. 4](https://www.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2025/0421/2025042100203.pdf); [Horizon Robotics FY2025 results, 19 Mar 2026](https://iis.aastocks.com/20260319/12059245-0.PDF)). Even with 50%+ revenue growth, the company is unlikely to be operating-profitable before 2027 on consensus estimates. Mitigant: RMB 15.4 bn of cash at end-2024 ([2024 年報, p. 10](https://www.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2025/0421/2025042100203.pdf)) provides multi-year runway.

**Valuation / multiple-compression risk (material).** TTM P/S of ~28× is more than 8× Mobileye's 3.4× and ~2× Black Sesame's 22×, both Horizon's closest comps. The valuation discounts (a) 30%+ continued top-line growth into 2028, (b) HSD success in urban NOA, and (c) CARIZON international monetization. If any one narrative breaks (a single quarter of revenue miss, a HSD-SOP slip, or a CARIZON program issue), the multiple could compress to ~12–15× P/S, implying ~50% downside even on unchanged revenue forecasts. The 2024 share-price range from HK$3.99 IPO to >HK$10 peak to HK$7.64 today shows the volatility this multiple-elasticity creates.

**Convertible-debt fair-value volatility.** The CARIAD convertible loan and other financial-liability components have generated multi-billion-RMB swings in reported net income (RMB +4,677 m in FY2024, RMB −3,407 m in H1-2025) ([2024 年報, p. 10](https://www.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2025/0421/2025042100203.pdf); [2025 中期報告, p. 9](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2025/0929/2025092901157.pdf)). Reported earnings will continue to be unreliable until these liabilities convert.

### Macroeconomic Risks

**China consumer / EV cycle sensitivity.** Horizon's growth is leveraged to Chinese passenger-vehicle volumes and to ADAS-attach rates. A sharp downturn in Chinese consumer auto demand (housing-wealth feedback, employment shock) would slow design-in revenue and per-vehicle royalty streams.

**FX exposure.** Horizon reports in RMB but holds significant USD-denominated convertible debt (CARIAD) and pays for foundry services in USD. Major RMB-USD moves affect both reported earnings (mark-to-market) and supplier costs.

**Geopolitical / China-Hong Kong listing risk.** Although primary-listed in Hong Kong, Horizon is exposed to any escalation of US-China technology-decoupling, Hong Kong capital-control changes, or restrictions on US investor ownership of Chinese AI / semiconductor names.
