### Neutral Analyst:

Both sides are mostly right and partly wrong, which is exactly why the trader's Hold call is the correct base case but the supporting structure needs sharpening. The Aggressive analyst is right that **the operational catalysts haven't fully played out** — the OpenAI MI450 ramp is contracted ([AMD–OpenAI 8-K Ex. 99.1, 2025-10-06](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2488/000119312525230895/d28189dex991.htm)) and execution proof keeps stacking (2nm Venice EPYC first HPC product on TSMC's 2nm — [Simply Wall St · 2026-05-22](https://finance.yahoo.com/sectors/technology/articles/latest-news-ai-chips-amd-113829305.html); AMD 27.4% server CPU unit share — [Simply Wall St · 2026-05-16](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/amd-server-share-gains-test-131545971.html); Helios + Celestica + Riot + Amkor partnership stack). The Conservative analyst is right that **the insider distribution + dilution overhang is the cleanest primary-source bear signal** and that the BofA crowdedness survey is a structural risk premium that doesn't reward incremental adds. The disagreement boils down to *which information is more load-bearing for the next 3–6 months*. My read: in the **near term** (Q2-FY2026 print early August), the bear data points dominate because tape-positioning unwinds before fundamentals catch up — but in the **medium term** (2H 2026 MI450 + OpenAI go-live), the bull data points re-assert because contracted backlog converts to revenue regardless of multiple compression in the interim. That argues for: maintain existing positions (the Aggressive is right that exiting forfeits the contracted backlog), don't add at $449 (the Conservative is right that the risk-adjusted return at this price is materially worse than at $300 or $370), and *don't* tighten the stop to $395 because that's where 10-day momentum selling tends to flush before the print and you'd get whipsawed out before the 2H catalyst. The trader's $370 stop is more durable. **Buy protective puts dated 2026-09 — that's the Conservative's best suggestion** — because it gives downside coverage through the Q2 print without sacrificing the MI450 milestone upside; the budget should be ~25 bps of premium, sized as 50-delta on half the position. On the Aggressive's call to add: I'd defer that until either (a) a Section 16 officer makes an open-market *buy* at ≥$400 (a primary-source confirmation that the insider distribution cluster has reversed) or (b) the FTSE Russell rebalance flow lands and the stock has held the $440 level for 5+ sessions. Net call: **Hold the trader's plan, but layer in put protection and respect the asymmetry — the next 90 days favor the bear, the next 12 months favor the bull, and you don't get paid enough to bridge that gap with a naked long at $449**.
