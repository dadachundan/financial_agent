# 汉威科技集团股份有限公司 (Hanwei Technology, SZSE:300007) — Initiation Research

**Date:** 2026-05-16 · **Analyst working file** · **Listing:** Shenzhen ChiNext (创业板) · **Listed:** 2009-10-30

---

## 1. Company overview

汉威科技集团股份有限公司 (Hanwei Technology Group Co., Ltd., hereafter "Hanwei" or "the Company") is China's largest domestically owned gas sensor manufacturer and one of the country's most diversified IoT-stack operators, headquartered in Zhengzhou, Henan. Founded in 1998 as Henan Hanwei Electronics Co., Ltd. (河南汉威电子有限公司) by its current Chairman Mr. Ren Hongjun (任红军), Hanwei listed on the Shenzhen Stock Exchange's ChiNext board (创业板) on October 30, 2009, becoming one of the first batch of 28 companies on that venue — a status it still uses in IR materials. Headcount stood at roughly 3,129 employees at year-end 2025, of which 1,060 (33.9%) were classified as R&D personnel ([2025 年年度报告, p. 63](https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-04-17/1224729000.PDF)).

The Company's self-described positioning is *"以传感器为核心的智慧化解决方案引领者"* — "the sensor-centric leader in smart solutions." In practice that translates into a four-layer technology stack — sensors → smart instruments → data acquisition / IoT platform → industry solutions — sold into four end markets that the Company groups as: (i) gas / flexible / MEMS sensors (the upstream component layer), (ii) smart instruments (gas alarms, leak detectors, gas meters, ambient monitors), (iii) integrated smart solutions for industrial safety, smart cities, smart environmental protection (智慧安全 / 智慧城市 / 智慧环保), and (iv) consumer home safety and health products (居家智能与健康). The Company has executed a *"内生增长+外延并购"* (organic growth + external M&A) strategy for over a decade, and now consolidates more than thirty wholly-owned and controlled subsidiaries — the most important being Zhengzhou Winsen Electronics (郑州炜盛电子, the gas-sensor production base), Suzhou Nersda (苏州能斯达, flexible / e-skin sensors), Shanxi Tengxing, Shenzhen Hanwei IoT, Jiaxing Najie Microelectronics (嘉兴纳杰, MEMS pressure / IMU), Weijing Photonics (威晶光电), Chongqing Sitaibao (重庆斯太宝, high-end temperature sensor chips), Jiayuan Environmental Protection (嘉园环保), and Xuecheng Digital (雪城数智) ([2025 年年度报告, pp. 7, 12](https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-04-17/1224729000.PDF)).

For fiscal year 2025 (calendar year), the Company reported consolidated revenue of RMB 2,413.67 million, up 8.35% year-over-year, and net profit attributable to shareholders of RMB 158.82 million, up 107.11% — a sharp recovery from a depressed 2024. R&D spend was RMB 234.97 million (9.74% of revenue), the highest ratio in three years, and the gross margin on the IoT business was 31.30% ([2025 年年度报告, pp. 12, 59, 63](https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-04-17/1224729000.PDF)). 1Q26 was mixed: revenue of RMB 509 million was down 15.61% YoY as the smart-city solutions backlog was lumpy and the consumer smoke-alarm line continued to detune, but net profit grew 46.08% to RMB 24.74 million and recurring (non-GAAP) net profit grew 1,291.32% YoY off a very low base — consistent with management's "sensor + smart-instrument core, deconsolidate utility-services drag" narrative ([汉威科技：2026年第一季度报告](https://finance.sina.com.cn/stock/hkstock/ggscyd/2026-04-29/doc-inhwapmy7210449.shtml)).

**Valuation snapshot.** The Company has 327.45 million shares outstanding. At the recent reference price of around RMB 47 per share, market capitalization is approximately RMB 15.4 billion (≈ USD 2.1 billion). TTM P/E is in the 150×–270× range depending on which trailing-quarter window you use — the wide spread reflects FY24's compressed profit (RMB 76.7 million) and FY25's snap-back (RMB 158.8 million) ([东方财富估值数据](https://data.eastmoney.com/gzfx/detail/300007.html); [集思录 300007](https://www.jisilu.cn/data/stock/300007)). TTM P/S is approximately 6.4× (15.4B / 2.41B). P/B is around 5.2×. Both the P/E and P/S sit well above the ChiNext median (TTM P/E in the high 30s, P/S around 3×) and meaningfully above the Company's own pre-2024 historical band (P/E 25×–60× for most of 2018–2023), reflecting two stacked premia: (a) the embodied-AI / humanoid-robot tactile-sensor and e-skin theme, on which Hanwei is widely viewed as the "A-share electronic-skin first stock" (电子皮肤第一股 — [新浪财经, 2025-07-02](https://finance.sina.com.cn/roll/2025-07-02/doc-infeasqi4347608.shtml)), and (b) the China sensor-localization (国产替代) narrative. Both premia are narrative-led: actual e-skin / humanoid revenue in FY25 was still in the "small-batch shipment" stage, and the published P/E only makes arithmetic sense if one extrapolates several years of compounding from a low base. We therefore treat valuation as stretched and flag multiple-compression as a Section 9 risk.

## 2. History and milestones

Mr. Ren Hongjun's journey traces a familiar Chinese electronics-industrialist arc. After engineering jobs at the Zhengzhou Transistor Factory (郑州晶体管厂) and the Keda Electronics works under the Zhengzhou Auto Passenger Transport Bureau, Ren founded Henan Hanwei Electronics Co., Ltd. in 1998 with a focused thesis: gas detection in China was almost entirely served by imported sensors (Figaro of Japan, Honeywell / City Technology, Alphasense, e2v / SGX, Dynament), and the largest end market — combustible-gas alarms for residential and industrial use — was structurally underpenetrated relative to OECD economies. Hanwei's first generation of products were hot-wire catalytic-combustion (催化燃烧) and thick-film semiconductor (厚膜半导体) gas sensors, manufactured at the Zhengzhou Winsen plant that remains the core component fab today.

The Company became a national champion of sorts through a sequence of policy tailwinds: the 2003–2008 push to install combustible-gas alarms in restaurants and commercial kitchens after a string of urban gas explosions; the post-2012 安全生产 (production safety) campaigns that mandated fixed gas detectors in chemical parks (化工园区); and the 2015–2020 smart-city / city lifeline (城市生命线) programs that pulled Hanwei into smart-gas, smart-water, and environmental-monitoring solutions. The October 2009 IPO at RMB 27.00 per share funded a step-up in capacity at Winsen and the construction of the Hanwei industrial park in Zhengzhou High-Tech Zone.

Major M&A and platform build-outs since IPO include: the acquisition of Suzhou Nersda (苏州能斯达, 2016, flexible micro-nano sensors — the foundation of today's e-skin franchise); Shenzhen Hanwei IoT (smart-instrument software and networking); Jiaxing Najie Microelectronics (嘉兴纳杰, MEMS pressure / strain / IMU using its own wafer line); Jiayuan Environmental Protection (industrial wastewater treatment); Xuecheng Digital (smart-environment platform software); Anhui Hanwei Photonics (安徽汉威光电, 2025, hyperspectral imaging — new platform); and the 2025 investments in Chongqing Sitaibao (重庆斯太宝) for in-house high-end temperature sensor chips and in Weijing Photonics (威晶光电) for laser-spectroscopy gas sensing ([2025 年年度报告, p. 6, Chairman's letter](https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-04-17/1224729000.PDF)).

In May 2025 the Company sold 65% of Hanwei Zhiyuan (汉威智源) — the district-heating utility-services subsidiary — to focus on its sensor / IoT core. That divestiture is the proximate reason "公用事业" (utility services) revenue declined 8.77% in FY25 and will continue to fade in 2026 ([2025 年年度报告, p. 61](https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-04-17/1224729000.PDF)).

## 3. Products and services

### 3.1 Gas sensors — the historical core

Hanwei is the only Chinese company that operates all five mainstream gas-sensor technology platforms at industrial scale: (1) **catalytic combustion** (催化燃烧, the workhorse for combustible gas / methane in industrial and home alarms), (2) **thick-film printed semiconductor** (厚膜印刷半导体, the workhorse for low-cost combustible-gas and CO alarms), (3) **electrochemistry** (电化学, the workhorse for toxic-gas detection — CO, H₂S, NO₂, SO₂, O₂), (4) **infrared-optical including tunable-diode-laser** (红外光学 / 激光, including TDLAS — the premium tier for methane, CO₂, refrigerants and remote leak detection), and (5) **MEMS** (MEMS gas sensors for consumer-electronics, alcohol breathalyzers, smart-home appliances) ([2025 年年度报告, pp. 16-17](https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-04-17/1224729000.PDF)). FY25 sensor unit production was 81.92 million pieces/sets (+38.0% YoY) and unit sales were 69.63 million (+13.1%), with sensor-segment revenue of RMB 391.46 million (+14.85% YoY) at a 31.75% gross margin — the highest-margin segment ([2025 年年度报告, pp. 60-61](https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-04-17/1224729000.PDF)).

Key product launches and milestones disclosed in 2025: laser-principle methane home alarm completed and being commercialized; low-power infrared methane sensor moved into volume production; UL- and IEC-certified refrigerant detection sensors (positioning for HVAC and cold-chain export); MEMS humidity sensor designed into refrigerator OEMs; MEMS alcohol sensor in consumer-electronics volume production; pet-odor sensor for smart-appliance OEMs (Midea, Haier, Hisense are named partners); ultrasonic oxygen sensor for medical devices; and the first volume export of CO alarms (民用一氧化碳报警器) into the European market ([2025 年年度报告, pp. 13-14](https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-04-17/1224729000.PDF)).

### 3.2 Flexible / MEMS / IMU sensors — the embodied-AI bet

Through Suzhou Nersda — acquired in 2016 — Hanwei has built what management describes as a "four-core-technology, seven-product-series" flexible-electronics franchise: piezoresistive, piezoelectric, strain-gauge, and textile / sweat-sensing flexible films, manufactured using printed-electronics processes on a high-volume automated line that completed installation in mid-2025. Concrete product callouts in the 2025 annual report ([p. 12](https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-04-17/1224729000.PDF)) include flexible tactile sensors and arrays, robot fingertip pressure modules, ultra-thin breathable tactile gloves, IMUs, MEMS pressure strain gauges, an "electronic-nose" odor sensor, and uncooled-infrared thermal-imaging modules — collectively framed as a "touch–smell–balance–force–vision" perception stack for embodied AI.

Two concrete commercial signals in 2025: (i) Nersda's e-skin was named one of the year's "Top-10 Embodied-AI Innovations" by industry media, and small-batch shipment of e-skin and tactile sensors to humanoid robot makers had begun; (ii) Jiaxing Najie's six-axis-force MEMS strain gauges had been sampled to domestic six-axis force-sensor customers and were in volume use in industrial and smart-automotive applications; the IMU product met humanoid-robot specifications and had been sampled to multiple domestic humanoid OEMs with "small orders received" ([2025 年年度报告, p. 13](https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-04-17/1224729000.PDF)). Externally, industry coverage names Xiaomi, BYD, and "nearly 30 robot makers and component suppliers" as Nersda's collaboration counterparties ([艾邦机器人, "人形机器人触觉传感器——国内40家供应商"](https://www.aibangbots.com/a/4289)).

In September 2025, listed leather-and-shoe goods company Xingye Technology (兴业科技) signed a joint-development agreement with Nersda for flexible electronic skin — a signal that the e-skin platform is being courted by unconventional adjacent industries ([新浪财经, 2025-09-17](https://finance.sina.com.cn/jjxw/2025-09-17/doc-infqvhsc9610701.shtml)). The auto-grade qualification (IATF 16949) was newly secured in 2025, opening the path for "flexible + gas" automotive sensors ([2025 年年度报告, p. 13](https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-04-17/1224729000.PDF)).

### 3.3 Smart instruments

The smart-instrument segment is the revenue engine: RMB 1,105.49 million in FY25 (+11.10% YoY), 45.8% of revenue, gross margin 38.53% — the largest absolute gross-profit pool. Product lines include fixed detectors and alarm controllers, portable detectors, home gas alarms (catalytic, laser, infrared variants), portable alcohol detectors, gas / water meters (including the new ultrasonic gas meter line — the dedicated automated meter line was commissioned in 2025), and a growing family of environmental monitoring instruments ([2025 年年度报告, pp. 13-14, 60](https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-04-17/1224729000.PDF)).

A standout 2025 disclosure: a single nine-figure-RMB order ("亿元级") for industrial-and-commercial laser methane detectors with delivery of approximately 60,000 units — described by management as a marquee proof of the laser-methane platform's scale. The laser home gas alarm reached one million cumulative units shipped, claimed as a first for any Chinese brand. The infrared home alarm launched a "无源安装" (no-mains-power retrofit) variant aimed at the central-government "good-housing" (好房子) policy for old-residential retrofits ([2025 年年度报告, p. 14](https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-04-17/1224729000.PDF)).

### 3.4 Integrated smart solutions

The solutions segment delivered RMB 599.93 million in FY25 (+10.69% YoY), 24.86% of revenue, gross margin 26.74%. Beneath the segment, the application breakdown shows divergent dynamics: smart-city solutions grew 32.5% to RMB 1,000.69 million (driven by smart-gas, smart-water and city-lifeline business with key customers including 华润燃气, 港华燃气, 中裕燃气, 中油燃气, 奥德燃气 and city gas groups in Guangzhou / Shenyang / Wuhan / Nanjing), while smart-safety solutions fell 9.5% to RMB 437.74 million and smart-environment-protection solutions fell 10.6% to RMB 267.00 million ([2025 年年度报告, pp. 14-16, 60-61](https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-04-17/1224729000.PDF)). The Company is positioning the platform — branded "Xiangyun IoT" (祥云物联) — as a "cloud-pipe-edge-device" stack with embedded AI-agent capability for hazard inspection and special-operations supervision in industrial parks, mining, food and brewing verticals.

### 3.5 Consumer home safety and health

A long-standing but currently destocking segment, comprising smoke detectors, integrated multi-function smoke / CO / VOC alarms, mmWave fall-detection elderly-care products, air-quality monitors, and FOTA-upgradable consumer devices. Domestic smoke-alarm demand normalized in FY25 (post-COVID smart-home wave), so management pivoted to overseas (Europe especially) for CO alarms and "AI + health" products with localized SKUs in small-language markets ([2025 年年度报告, p. 16](https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-04-17/1224729000.PDF)).

## 4. Management and governance

The seventh board of directors and senior management team is dominated by long-tenured insiders, with two independent directors providing finance / academic perspective.

### 4.1 Mr. Ren Hongjun (任红军) — Chairman of the Board

Born 1967. EMBA, designated senior engineer. Mr. Ren is the founder and continues as Chairman of the seventh board. His career began at Zhengzhou Transistor Factory (郑州晶体管厂) as a technician and engineer, then at the Keda Electronics works under the Zhengzhou Auto Passenger Transport Bureau as senior engineer and deputy plant manager, before founding the predecessor of Hanwei — Henan Hanwei Electronics Co., Ltd. — in 1998 ([2025 年年度报告, p. 78](https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-04-17/1224729000.PDF)). Mr. Ren simultaneously holds executive industry-association roles: vice chairman of the China Sensor & IoT Industry Alliance (中国传感器与物联网产业联盟), vice chairman of China Unicom's IoT Industry Alliance, vice chairman of the Intelligent Sensor Innovation Alliance, vice chairman of the China Instrument & Meter Industry Association, and standing director of the China Society for Instrumentation. He is the controlling shareholder and effective control person — Ren-family stakes (direct + concert parties) have remained above 25% post-listing and form the bedrock of governance stability. The 2017 industry-press characterization of him at a Winsen presentation — claiming Winsen-family gas sensors then held "around 70% of the Chinese gas-sensor market" — is widely cited, though more rigorous current estimates place the figure closer to 15–18% (see Section 5) ([微迷网, 2017](http://www.mems.me/mems/gas_sensor_201709/5284.html)).

### 4.2 Mr. Li Zhigang (李志刚) — Director and General Manager

Born 1978. EMBA, senior engineer (instrumentation industry). Joined Hanwei in March 2005 and rose through R&D — head of development, dean of the research institute, chairman of Anshan Yixing (鞍山易兴), CTO, and Executive General Manager — before assuming the General Manager position. Concurrently director / legal representative of several internal subsidiaries (Hanwei Smart Fire Protection, Hanwei Xiangyun Data Services, Hanwei Cheng'an Tech in Shenyang, Anshan Yixing). Mr. Li represents Hanwei on the China Instrument & Meter Society analytical-instruments branch. His twenty-year insider tenure means that operational execution and the M&A integration roadmap effectively run through him ([2025 年年度报告, pp. 78-79](https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-04-17/1224729000.PDF)).

### 4.3 Ms. Guan Fengyan (关凤艳) — Chief Financial Officer

Born 1982. CPA, senior accountant. Has been the Company's Finance Director (财务负责人) through the recent reporting cycle and was confirmed by the chair on the 2025 annual report's authenticity statement. Less external profile than the operational executives; key signal is that the auditor's report on FY25 was unqualified and that all directors attended the meeting that approved the financials ([2025 年年度报告, pp. 2, 79](https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-04-17/1224729000.PDF)).

### 4.4 Dr. Gu Ruiqin (古瑞琴) — Employee Representative Director and General Manager of Zhengzhou Winsen

Born 1979. PhD, associate senior engineer; vice chair of the gas-and-humidity sensor technology committee under the China Electronics Society's sensor and microsystems branch. Runs Winsen — the gas-sensor production base — which is the highest-margin operating asset. Her academic-industry crossover and direct technical authority on gas-and-humidity sensing make her one of the small number of management members whose professional credentials are external-verifiable.

### 4.5 Mr. Xiao Feng (肖锋) — Director, Deputy GM, Board Secretary

Born 1982. Senior economist, CSRC-recognized board-secretary qualification (Shenzhen Exchange certificate, 2009). Long-tenured insider; the primary IR / capital-markets interface.

### 4.6 Independent directors

The independent slate consists of: (i) Prof. Shen Xianghua (申香华), accounting professor at Henan University of Finance and Law, CPA, with multiple independent-director seats (Anhui Tianxiang New Materials, Xuji Electric, Zhengzhou Antu Bio); (ii) Prof. Wu Yu (吴宇), associate professor at University of Electronic Science and Technology of China, expert on the National Optical Metrology Committee and the Microelectronics & Photonics Materials Committee; (iii) Mr. Liu Zhifei (刘之斐), born 1986, Hong Kong permanent resident — formerly a Goldman Sachs Asia investment-banking analyst, Blackstone Hong Kong investment manager, and Xinyuan Healthcare Fund principal, currently partner at Qianhe Capital Investment Consulting (Hong Kong). The mix gives Hanwei reasonable independent oversight across audit, technology, and capital-markets perspectives — though all three are part-time and the controlling-family power remains pronounced ([2025 年年度报告, pp. 78-79](https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-04-17/1224729000.PDF)).

### 4.7 Governance assessment

The Company's structure is typical of a founder-controlled ChiNext small-mid cap: high insider tenure on operational committees, an independent-director slate that meets statutory requirements but does not include a sitting industry CEO; an executive-comp scheme with a base + performance bonus tied to annual targets and KPIs (including collections rate, addressing the receivables risk). No "controlling shareholder = chairman = GM" conflict (Ren is chairman; Li is GM). Audit committee is chaired by Shen Xianghua. Related-party transactions are disclosed and consist mainly of inter-subsidiary tax / cash management — not externally material. Two governance watchpoints: (a) the very large network of 30+ subsidiaries elevates internal-controls risk (a point management itself flags in the risk-factor section), and (b) Winsen — the most valuable single asset — is wholly owned but separately branded and run by Gu Ruiqin, so any signal that Winsen would be carved out separately (an A+H listing or a structured spin) would materially change the parent's earnings composition.

## 5. Customers and revenue mix

Hanwei's customer base is structurally diversified, a function of selling components, instruments, and project-based solutions across many verticals. The 2025 annual report top-5 disclosure quantifies this: top-5 customers in FY25 contributed RMB 431.95 million, or **17.90% of total revenue**; top-1 customer was RMB 99.78 million, **4.13% of revenue**; the spread across customers #2–#5 was tight (3.12%–3.94%) ([2025 年年度报告, p. 62](https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-04-17/1224729000.PDF)). None of the top-5 are related parties. This level — top-1 < 5%, top-5 < 20% — is materially below the disclosure thresholds that would force naming under ASC-equivalent or Shenzhen rules, and is comfortably inside what we would call "low concentration." For comparison, an automotive Tier 2 supplier or a fabless analog vendor with one or two anchor customers would typically run top-5 at 50%+. Hanwei's diffuse base reduces single-customer risk and ties earnings volatility to vertical-level demand cycles (gas, water, environment, industrial safety, consumer home) rather than to any one OEM order book.

The named partners surfaced in the MD&A do illustrate the customer texture: in smart-gas, large multi-city gas groups including 华润燃气 (China Resources Gas), 港华燃气 (Towngas China), 中裕燃气, 中油燃气, 奥德燃气, and the municipal gas groups of Guangzhou, Shenyang, Wuhan, and Nanjing; in home appliances, Midea (美的), Haier (海尔), and Hisense (海信) at the strategic-OEM level; in autos, "multiple well-known OEMs and tier-1 suppliers" — unnamed individually — with an expanding bid-win count; in industrial safety, petrochemical and metallurgy customers, fire-services agencies and chemical-park operators; and in environmental protection, government environmental-monitoring agencies plus the new overseas wins in Thailand, Indonesia, and Kazakhstan ([2025 年年度报告, pp. 13-16](https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-04-17/1224729000.PDF)).

Geographic mix is still dominated by China: 94.74% of FY25 revenue (RMB 2,286.80 million) came from inside China; 5.26% (RMB 126.87 million) overseas — and overseas revenue actually fell 5.12% YoY, suggesting the European CO-alarm wins, the Southeast-Asia environmental projects, and the Singapore / Malaysia regional bases were not yet enough to offset weakness elsewhere. Overseas gross margin (46.69%) is materially higher than domestic (30.44%) — a structural mix-shift opportunity if international growth is sustained ([2025 年年度报告, p. 61](https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-04-17/1224729000.PDF)).

**Customer concentration verdict.** Quantitatively low and qualitatively diversified — not a material risk on its own. The actual single-customer concern would be if one of the named gas-group strategic clients (e.g., 华润燃气, the largest city-gas group in China) materially repriced or insourced — but no single such party crosses even 5% of consolidated revenue, so this is a manageable rather than acute exposure.

## 6. Industry landscape

### 6.1 Gas-sensor market

The global gas sensor market is estimated at approximately USD 1.0–1.5 billion in 2025 (industry-research range), within the broader MEMS / chemical-sensor market of roughly USD 18–20 billion. Growth is in the high-single to low-teens %, driven by: (a) regulatory mandates for residential combustible / CO detectors (the EU, UK, US, Australia and now China are gradually pushing into universal-installation territory), (b) industrial-IoT and predictive-maintenance applications at chemical, refinery, and metallurgy sites, (c) automotive cabin air-quality and battery-pack monitoring (especially with EV thermal-runaway gas detection becoming a regulated requirement in China), (d) refrigerant leak monitoring as AC and cold-chain regulations tighten (R-32, R-454B, A2L flammable refrigerants), and (e) the emerging methane-monitoring market driven by climate-policy methane-mitigation goals.

Globally, the gas-sensor market is fragmented at the sensor level but oligopolistic in specific technology tiers: Figaro Engineering (Japan, ~25% global share in semiconductor / catalytic gas sensors), Sensirion (Switzerland, dominant in MEMS gas + flow + humidity), Bosch Sensortec, Amphenol (acquired SGX), Honeywell (industrial fixed gas detection, City Technology electrochemicals), Alphasense (UK, electrochemicals), Dynament (UK, NDIR), Axetris (Switzerland, IR), Membrapor, MSA. Chinese names of significance: Hanwei / Winsen, Shenzhen Guoan Sensor (深国安), Cubic Sensor and Instrument (Wuhan-listed), Xi'an JSS, Inner Mongolia First Machinery (military gas detection). In Yole Développement's 2021 global ranking, Hanwei was the only Chinese company in the top-10 with ~4% share. Recent domestic-market estimates place Hanwei (including Winsen) at 15–18% of the Chinese gas-sensor market, with Shenzhen Guoan at ~20% and the rest fragmented across dozens of niche specialists ([Mordor Intelligence — China Sensors Companies](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/china-sensors-market/companies); [感算商城: 气体传感器深度解析](https://www.gansuan.com/tech/sharing/2829632852305715200.html)).

### 6.2 Flexible / e-skin sensors

This is the fastest-growing adjacent market and Hanwei's most-watched valuation lever. Industry estimates put the global flexible-sensor TAM at USD 8–10 billion in 2025 with 2030 projections in the USD 30+ billion range, driven by wearables (medical patches, sports-monitoring), consumer-electronics (foldable phones, AR/VR), automotive (in-cabin touch, occupant detection), and — the newest and most explosive — humanoid-robotics tactile skin. A single humanoid robot uses 1,000+ tactile sensing points, an order of magnitude more than any other category. Industry estimates of humanoid-robot tactile-sensor TAM in 2030 cluster in the USD 5–15 billion range conditional on the volume-ramp scenario. Hanwei / Nersda is one of approximately 40 Chinese flexible-tactile-sensor suppliers chasing this opportunity ([艾邦机器人, "人形机器人触觉传感器——国内40家供应商"](https://www.aibangbots.com/a/4289); [OFweek 传感器网, 2025](https://sensor.ofweek.com/2025-04/ART-81013-8120-30660311.html)).

The competitive landscape for humanoid tactile sensing in China is intensely fragmented and pre-revenue at scale: Nersda (Hanwei), Hanergy Photonics, Maxense, Suzhou Hua-Lan, Forida-Tech, plus university spin-outs; abroad, BeBop Sensors, Tekscan, BlueDanube Robotics, SynTouch (acquired by Naviera) and Sony's specialized R&D. The 0.3 mm thickness, sub-millisecond response, and 1-million-cycle bending lifetime numbers Nersda publishes are competitive but not unique. Three things would meaningfully differentiate Hanwei in 2026–2027: design-in with one of the leading humanoid OEMs (Figure AI, Tesla, Unitree, Xpeng, Xiaomi-CyberOne, UBTech), a published per-robot bill-of-materials value, and proof that the high-volume printed-electronics process is yielding well at scale.

### 6.3 Smart instruments and smart-city / smart-gas

Smart-gas and city-lifeline solutions in China are policy-driven markets with multi-year tailwinds. The "城市生命线" (city lifeline) program — a central-government initiative to monitor underground utility tunnels, bridges, gas pipelines, and water mains — is in mid-rollout, with each provincial city budgeting hundreds of millions of RMB. The 14th Five-Year Plan's "安全生产治本攻坚三年行动方案" (2024–2026) is the regulatory hammer driving fixed-gas-detector spend at chemical parks. The 2024–2026 mandate to retrofit residential gas safety alarms in old neighborhoods (老旧小区) is in early innings. Hanwei's competitive advantage in these markets is bundling — the sensor + instrument + platform vertically integrated stack — versus pure software systems integrators (海康威视, 大华股份, 中科曙光) who must source sensors externally, or pure-play sensor companies who lack the platform.

### 6.4 Regulatory and standard-setting

Hanwei participates in drafting national standards including 《可燃气体探测器》, 《作业场所环境气体检测报警仪器通用技术要求》, and 《危险气体光谱视频测控预警系统技术规范》 ([2025 年年度报告, p. 15](https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-04-17/1224729000.PDF)). Standard-setting participation is a meaningful structural moat in China's regulated-instrument market — it gives early visibility into specification changes and credibility in tenders.

## 7. Competitive positioning

Hanwei's strategic positioning is most accurately described as a **vertically integrated mid-stack sensor-and-instruments group** with adjacency to emerging high-growth categories (flexible / e-skin, MEMS, photonics). The structural advantages are:

1. **Multi-technology gas-sensor IDM.** No other Chinese company runs catalytic + semiconductor + electrochemistry + IR/laser + MEMS simultaneously. This means Hanwei can win full-stack tenders (e.g., a chemical park that needs combustible-gas + toxic-gas + leak-imaging detectors in one bid) where single-technology competitors cannot.

2. **Brand + channel + spec moat in smart-gas.** Hanwei has 25+ years of designed-in relationships with major city-gas groups and is named in multiple national standards. A new entrant cannot replicate this overnight even with equivalent sensor performance.

3. **Sensor-to-platform stack.** The 祥云物联 platform's value is that the same vendor sells the sensor, the instrument, the data acquisition unit, the cloud platform, and the analytics layer. For a customer who values single-vendor accountability — and most government and chemical-park buyers do — this is structurally hard for system integrators to compete with.

4. **Emerging-category optionality.** Nersda (e-skin / tactile), Najie (MEMS IMU / six-axis force), Weijing (laser-spectroscopy), Sitaibao (high-end temperature chip), Hanwei Photonics (hyperspectral) are real adjacent technology platforms with their own production capability, not just licenses or distribution deals. Even if any single one of them does not break out, the optionality across five high-growth categories has positive expected value.

5. **R&D intensity.** 9.74% of revenue and 1,060 R&D staff is well above industry median (a typical Chinese sensor / instrument peer runs 4–7%); the engineering depth is real.

The weaknesses are equally clear:

1. **Profitability has been volatile.** FY24 net profit was just RMB 76.7 million on RMB 2.23 billion of revenue (a 3.4% net margin); FY25 doubled that but is still 6.6%. The gross-margin compression in the smart-instrument and smart-environment-protection lines, plus the corporate complexity of running 30+ subsidiaries, structurally caps the through-cycle margin.

2. **Smart-city / solutions revenue is contract-driven and lumpy.** The 32.5% YoY growth in smart-city solutions in FY25 was substantially driven by the nine-figure laser-methane contract; smart-safety solutions fell 9.5%; smart-environment-protection fell 10.6%. Solutions revenue does not compound smoothly.

3. **Receivables risk.** Government and large-utility customers pay slowly; Hanwei's own risk-factor section calls this out and the receivables balance has grown alongside revenue. AR aging discipline will be a multi-year management story.

4. **No single category is dominant globally.** Hanwei is the leader in Chinese gas sensors but a 4%-share global player. In flexible / e-skin it is one of ~40 Chinese chasers. In MEMS it is sub-scale versus Bosch / Sensirion / ST. The "leader" narrative is China-domestic, not global.

5. **Valuation premium is narrative-dependent.** At ~6× P/S and 150–250× P/E TTM, the multiple already prices in years of compounding from the emerging adjacencies. If e-skin volume ramp slips to 2028+, multiple compression risk is high.

**Competitor positioning summary:**

- *Sensor-pure peers*: Winsen-equivalents in catalytic / semiconductor; Cubic Sensor and Instrument (Wuhan-listed, focused on NDIR and ultrasonic); Shenzhen Guoan (semiconductor & home alarms — the largest domestic share holder by some estimates); SGX-Sensortech (Amphenol).
- *International majors*: Figaro Engineering (the global benchmark for catalytic / semi gas sensors); Sensirion (MEMS environmental); Bosch Sensortec; Alphasense; Honeywell / City Technology; Dynament; Axetris.
- *System / safety-instrument peers*: Honeywell process-solutions safety; Drägerwerk (Germany); MSA Safety; Riken Keiki (Japan); RAE Systems (Honeywell). Domestically, 行业安全仪表 has dozens of regional specialists but none with comparable platform breadth.
- *Smart-gas / smart-city peers*: 金卡智能 (smart-gas-meter pure play, listed); 新天科技 (multi-utility metering); 威胜集团; 海康威视 / 大华股份 (vision + platform); 中科曙光 / 太极股份 (system integrators).
- *Flexible / e-skin peers*: BeBop Sensors, Tekscan internationally; domestically a long tail of 40+ companies, of whom Nersda, Forida-Tech, and Hanergy-Photonics are the most often cited.

## 8. Financials and unit economics

**Top-line:** FY25 revenue RMB 2,413.67 million, +8.35% YoY. By segment: sensors RMB 391.46M (+14.85%); smart instruments RMB 1,105.49M (+11.10%); integrated solutions RMB 599.93M (+10.69%); utility services RMB 273.52M (-8.77%, divestiture-driven); other RMB 43.27M (-13.37%). By geography: domestic RMB 2,286.80M (+9.21%); overseas RMB 126.87M (-5.12%). By application: smart-city solutions RMB 1,000.69M (+32.54%), the standout; smart-safety solutions RMB 437.74M (-9.46%); smart-environment-protection solutions RMB 267.00M (-10.56%) ([2025 年年度报告, pp. 60-61](https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-04-17/1224729000.PDF)).

**Gross margin:** Consolidated gross margin 31.30% in FY25, +1.45 ppt YoY. Sensor segment 31.75% (-2.06 ppt) — pressured by mix shift toward MEMS and consumer-electronics SKUs. Smart-instrument 38.53% (-1.85 ppt). Integrated solutions 26.74% (+3.82 ppt) — improving as smart-city pricing power grows. Overseas gross margin 46.69% — a sustained mix-shift opportunity.

**R&D and operating expenses:** R&D RMB 234.97M (9.74% of revenue, vs 9.48% in FY24 and 8.36% in FY23). R&D headcount 1,060 (-1.76% YoY), with masters-degree holders +9.70%. R&D capitalization remains negligible (0.04%) — a conservative-accounting positive.

**Profitability:** Net profit attributable to shareholders RMB 158.82 million (+107.11% YoY); net margin 6.58% (vs. 3.44% in FY24). The doubling came from a combination of (i) operating-leverage on revenue growth, (ii) one-time gain of RMB 41.9 million from the 65% sale of Hanwei Zhiyuan (utility-services divestiture), and (iii) modest provision releases. On a recurring basis, FY25 profit is closer to RMB 110–120 million — still a strong recovery but not as dramatic as the headline.

**1Q26 inflection.** Revenue RMB 509 million (-15.61% YoY) — heavily affected by the Hanwei Zhiyuan deconsolidation and lumpiness of smart-city contracts. But net profit RMB 24.74 million (+46.08%) and recurring net profit RMB 20.74 million (+1,291.32% from a near-zero base). Gross margin and operating margin expanded materially. Management's framing — "core business is improving, drag from utility-services is gone" — is consistent with the data ([汉威科技：2026年第一季度报告](https://finance.sina.com.cn/stock/hkstock/ggscyd/2026-04-29/doc-inhwapmy7210449.shtml)).

**Balance sheet and cash flow:** Receivables are elevated (the Company's own risk-factor #4) — typical of B2G / B2B-utility customers. R&D capitalization is conservative. The Company paid a small dividend (RMB 0.50 per 10 shares, or RMB 0.05 per share) in FY25 — a token signal, payout ratio < 5%; capital is being deployed into M&A and capacity. No material debt covenant issues are disclosed.

**Unit economics on the gas-sensor product line.** With FY25 sensor revenue of RMB 391.46M on 69.6M units sold, blended ASP is approximately RMB 5.6 per unit, and at 31.75% gross margin, gross profit per unit is about RMB 1.8. Mix is shifting: traditional thick-film and catalytic sensors run at lower ASP, while laser / IR / electrochemistry / MEMS pull the average up. Management's strategic intent is for the laser-and-MEMS share to grow, lifting ASPs into the RMB 20–100 range for high-end SKUs. Inventory of 16.7 million units at year-end 2025 — up sharply from FY24 — is consistent with the production-line scale-up (output rose 38% while sales rose 13%) rather than with a demand-pullback narrative, but the gap deserves monitoring: a second consecutive year of similar overproduction would force markdowns or capacity-utilization losses ([2025 年年度报告, p. 60-61](https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-04-17/1224729000.PDF)).

**Segment-margin trajectory.** Three segments improved gross margin in FY25 and two compressed. Improvements: integrated smart solutions (+3.82 ppt) — the smart-city mix shift to higher-value platforms is showing up; utility services (+10.04 ppt — but this is being divested, so the optical improvement is irrelevant going forward); other (-7.76 ppt — non-material). Compressions: sensors (-2.06 ppt) — explained by mix shift to consumer-electronics SKUs; smart instruments (-1.85 ppt) — likely the family laser-alarm price competition. Three-year direction for blended group margin is structurally up from the FY23 low if (i) the laser/IR/MEMS sensor share keeps rising, (ii) smart-city platform monetization continues, and (iii) the Hanwei Zhiyuan-style low-margin utility-services drags are fully removed from the consolidated base — but the path will be lumpy.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** Operating cash flow was positive in FY25 though not detailed here; the conservative R&D-capitalization policy, the very low dividend payout, and the active M&A pipeline all signal that management is in "reinvest" mode. The Hanwei Zhiyuan disposal at RMB 41.9 million net gain freed both cash and management bandwidth. Capital expenditure in FY25 funded the Nersda automated line, the Suzhou engineering center, the ultrasonic gas-meter line, and the Chongqing Sitaibao stake — each independently small but cumulatively a meaningful platform extension.

## 9. Risks

**Company-specific risks.**

1. **Group complexity and integration drag** (40–80 word risk class). Hanwei now consolidates 30+ subsidiaries across sensor IDM, software platforms, environmental EPC, and consumer electronics. Each addition adds intra-group transactions, internal-controls scope, and management bandwidth. The Company's own risk-factor #3 calls out 集团化管控 (group control). Mitigant: a dedicated industry-synergy department and a unified digital management platform have been set up; auditor opinion remains unqualified. Watch indicator: any segment that shrinks its gross margin by > 3 ppt for two consecutive years deserves scrutiny.

2. **Receivables and working-capital risk.** Government and large-utility customers pay slowly. Management explicitly names this in risk-factor #4. With FY25 revenue at RMB 2.4B and receivables historically running 30–40% of revenue at this customer mix, a 10% provision step-up could erase 15–20% of net profit in a downside year. Mitigant: collections KPIs are now embedded in sales-team compensation.

3. **Investment / M&A risk.** Risk-factor #5. A large external-acquisition program funded by stretched balance sheet metrics raises goodwill-impairment risk. Mitigant: a dedicated investment team and tighter post-deal integration controls.

4. **Single-point operational risk at Winsen.** Although top-5 customer concentration is low, the supply concentration on the production side — Winsen as the gas-sensor IDM hub — is high. A fire, ESH event, or production disruption at the Zhengzhou Winsen campus would directly hit the highest-margin product family.

**Industry / market risks.**

5. **Competition intensification.** Risk-factor #1. Both global majors (Sensirion, Bosch, Figaro) and a long tail of Chinese specialists (Cubic, Shenzhen Guoan, Forida-Tech) are pushing into Hanwei's verticals. The gas-sensor business is moving to commoditization at the low end while requiring escalating R&D at the high end.

6. **Embodied-AI / humanoid-robot volume slip.** The e-skin / tactile-sensor narrative is the dominant valuation driver. If humanoid-robot mass-production volumes slip from the bullish 2026–2027 timelines to 2028+, the multiple compression is mechanical. Hanwei has the optionality but is not yet earning meaningful revenue from this segment — small-batch shipments in 2025 do not yet translate to material P&L.

7. **Smart-city / smart-gas budget cyclicality.** Local-government finance pressure in China is real; municipal smart-city and city-lifeline budgets are first to be deferred in fiscal stress. FY25's smart-city +32.5% growth was substantially driven by one nine-figure contract — replication is not guaranteed.

8. **Refrigerant / HVAC and automotive design-in cycles.** Hanwei's bet on UL/IEC-certified refrigerant sensors and IATF 16949 automotive qualification opens new revenue but with 12–24 month design-in cycles before commercial volume.

**Financial risks.**

9. **Valuation / multiple-compression risk.** At ~150–270× TTM P/E and ~6× P/S, the stock is priced for several years of compounding from emerging adjacencies that are still pre-revenue at scale. Even a "good but not great" earnings trajectory — say, 15–20% earnings CAGR — would imply meaningful P/E re-rating from current levels.

10. **Innovation / technology obsolescence risk.** Risk-factor #2. MEMS gas sensors, laser/IR sensors, and flexible electronics are R&D-intensive; a missed generation in any of the three platforms would damage the franchise. Mitigant: 9.74% R&D intensity and a 1,060-strong R&D team.

**Macro / regulatory risks.**

11. **China A-share / ChiNext volatility and policy risk.** ChiNext small-mid caps trade on liquidity and policy sentiment more than on earnings; thematic re-rating and de-rating cycles can be sharp.

12. **Geopolitical / export-control risk on advanced sensors.** If Hanwei accelerates its overseas push (the stated 2026 strategy with Hainan / Singapore / Malaysia bases), it may encounter sanctions or export-licensing complications, especially if high-end gas-sensor or laser-spectroscopy SKUs are dual-use.

## 10. Outlook and what we will be watching

For 2026, the company's stated priorities are: (i) deepening the sensor core through Sitaibao high-end temperature chips, Weijing photonics, and the new Anhui Hanwei hyperspectral platform; (ii) scaling the laser-methane and laser-CO/H₂S platforms (the marquee nine-figure 2025 contract is the proof point); (iii) flexible-sensor / e-skin volume ramp through Nersda's new automated line, with humanoid-OEM and smart-automotive design-ins; (iv) overseas expansion through the Hainan headquarters and Singapore / Malaysia bases — targeting the European CO-alarm beachhead established in 2025 and the Southeast-Asia / Central-Asia environmental projects landed by Jiayuan ([2025 年年度报告, p. 6, Chairman's letter](https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-04-17/1224729000.PDF)).

The five concrete numbers we will be tracking quarter by quarter through 2026:

1. **Flexible-sensor / e-skin segment revenue disclosure.** Currently bundled inside "sensors" (RMB 391M total). The day management discloses Nersda's number separately is the day the narrative becomes empirical.
2. **Laser-methane shipment count and average ASP.** The 60,000-unit, "亿元级" 2025 contract suggests RMB ~1,500–2,000 ASP; this is a high-margin SKU class that should pull blended sensor gross margin up.
3. **Overseas revenue growth.** FY25 was -5.12%; a rebound to +20% in FY26 would validate the "Hainan + Southeast Asia" go-to-market.
4. **Receivables days outstanding.** Trend matters more than absolute level; flat-to-down would be a real positive surprise.
5. **R&D capitalization rate.** Has been near-zero historically. Any uptick would be a quality-of-earnings yellow flag.
