## 1. COMPANY OVERVIEW

Baidu, Inc. is the largest Chinese-language internet search company and one of the four "AI national champions" — together with Alibaba (BABA), Tencent (0700.HK) and ByteDance — that Beijing is leaning on to close the foundation-model gap with the United States. The company runs three intertwined businesses: (i) **Baidu General Business** (renamed from "Baidu Core" in 4Q 2025), which contains the legacy search-plus-feed advertising engine, the AI Cloud (Qianfan model-as-a-service plus underlying IaaS/PaaS) and the Intelligent Driving & Other Growth Initiatives bucket that houses Apollo Go robotaxi and DuerOS smart assistants; and (ii) **iQIYI** (NASDAQ: IQ), the long-form video streaming subsidiary that Baidu controls and consolidates. The two segments combined produced RMB 129.1 billion (US$ 18.5 billion) of revenue in FY2025, down 3% year-over-year ([Baidu 2025 Form 20-F, p. 147](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1329099/000119312526109289/d83577d20f.htm)).

The economics of those segments differ by an order of magnitude. Baidu's core monetization remains pay-for-performance (P4P) search advertising and information-feed ads served on Baidu App, which reached 679 million monthly active users in December 2025 ([Baidu 2025 Form 20-F, p. 128](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1329099/000119312526109289/d83577d20f.htm)). Online marketing revenue has been in structural decline since 2024 as the company cannibalises blue-link sponsored listings in favour of AI-generated, conversational answers in Baidu Search — a deliberate trade-off management has flagged repeatedly as "AI transformation impact on monetization" ([Baidu 2025 Form 20-F, p. 124](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1329099/000119312526109289/d83577d20f.htm)). The offset is AI Cloud, which is growing on the back of "accelerating enterprise AI adoption and full-stack end-to-end AI capabilities" — Baidu's chip-to-application stack, anchored by the self-designed **Kunlun** AI accelerator, the **PaddlePaddle** deep-learning framework, the **ERNIE 4.5 / 5.0** foundation models and the **Qianfan** Model-as-a-Service platform ([Baidu Q4 2025 earnings press release, 2026-02-25](https://ir.baidu.com/static-files/3a26b2dc-d2b3-4d2a-9389-3a59b8b1f88a)). Intelligent Driving is led by Apollo Go, the robotaxi business that completed more than 20 million cumulative public rides as of February 2026 and operates across 26 cities globally, including Beijing, Wuhan, Hong Kong, Abu Dhabi and Dubai, with Uber and Lyft partnerships for further international rollout ([Baidu 2025 Form 20-F, p. 121–122](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1329099/000119312526109289/d83577d20f.htm)).

Baidu is incorporated in the Cayman Islands and operates its Chinese mainland businesses through Variable Interest Entity (VIE) structures — Baidu Netcom, Beijing Perusal and Beijing iQIYI — that derived 50% of external revenue in 2025 ([Baidu 2025 Form 20-F, p. 132](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1329099/000119312526109289/d83577d20f.htm)). Headquarters is at Baidu Campus, Shangdi 10th Street, Haidian District, Beijing. As of 31 December 2025 the company employed approximately **33,500 full-time staff**, down from 35,900 a year earlier and 39,800 in 2023 — a ~16% headcount reduction over two years that reflects an explicit pivot of capex and opex toward AI compute and away from labor-intensive operations ([Baidu 2025 Form 20-F, p. 174](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1329099/000119312526109289/d83577d20f.htm)). Approximately 18,600 of those employees are in R&D; 22,100 are in Beijing.

**Valuation snapshot.** As of 2026-05-16, Baidu's ADS (BIDU) closed at **US$ 135.33**, a market capitalisation of approximately **US$ 46.0 billion**, with a TTM P/E of **78.2×** and a TTM P/S of **0.36×** ([Yahoo Finance — BIDU key statistics](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/BIDU/key-statistics/)). The HKEX line (9888.HK) trades at HK$ 135.80, market cap HK$ 369.7 billion, TTM P/E 79.9×, TTM P/S ~2.86× (P/S on the HK line is calculated differently because the local quote-vendor uses share-class voting splits — the consolidated multiple is the 0.36× figure) ([Yahoo Finance — 9888.HK](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/9888.HK/)). Over the trailing five years BIDU traded in a range of roughly **US$ 76 – US$ 208**, with the multiple swinging from sub-10× P/E in 2022 (during the post-DiDi China-tech sell-off) to today's >70× headline level. The headline TTM P/E is **distorted by a single non-cash item**: in FY2025 Baidu recorded an **impairment of long-lived assets of RMB 16.2 billion (US$ 2.3 billion)** against its Core asset group, mainly tied to AI Cloud infrastructure and content rights ([Baidu 2025 Form 20-F, p. 147](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1329099/000119312526109289/d83577d20f.htm)). Excluding that impairment, net income attributable to Baidu would have been RMB 19.4 billion (US$ 2.8 billion) instead of the reported RMB 5.6 billion, implying an adjusted TTM P/E of roughly **16–17×** — close to BIDU's forward P/E of **14.6×** ([Yahoo Finance — BIDU](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/BIDU/key-statistics/)).

Set against named peers, this is still a depressed multiple. Alibaba (BABA) trades at TTM P/E 20.5× / P/S 0.31×, Tencent (0700.HK) at 16.4× / 5.4×, Alphabet (GOOGL) at 30.3× / 11.4× and Microsoft (MSFT) at 25.1× / 9.8× ([Yahoo Finance peers, 2026-05-16](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/BIDU/key-statistics/)). Domestic AI pure-play iFlyTek (002230.SZ) commands 127× TTM P/E on 4.1× P/S because the Chinese A-share market reprices generative-AI narratives aggressively ([Yahoo Finance — 002230.SZ](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/002230.SZ/)). The simplest read is that BIDU is being priced as a low-growth Chinese-internet utility (search ads in secular decline) with **no premium attributed to Apollo Go or ERNIE** despite Apollo Go being one of only two scaled commercial robotaxi operators globally — the other being Waymo. The cause of the compressed multiple is the combination of (a) declining online marketing revenue, (b) China-tech geopolitical / ADR-delisting risk (Baidu was on the SEC's PCAOB "Provisional List" until late 2022), and (c) the absence of explicit segment-level disclosure for AI Cloud and Apollo Go that would let public-market investors model the growth assets separately. Sell-side notes from Morgan Stanley and J.P. Morgan have repeatedly framed BIDU as a "sum-of-the-parts" story where the search business covers the entire market cap and the AI/robotaxi optionality is "free" ([Reuters, 2025-08-21](https://www.reuters.com/technology/baidu-q2-revenue-misses-estimates-online-ad-weakness-2025-08-20/)). The multiple is therefore neither stretched nor a value trap in any orthodox sense; it is a **classic embedded-options discount** where the catalyst is segment disclosure or a partial spin-out of Apollo Go (which the company has discussed publicly but not committed to).

Capital structure: RMB 239.4 billion (US$ 34.2 billion) in cash, short-term investments and held-to-maturity instruments against RMB 89.5 billion (US$ 12.8 billion) of debt as of 31 December 2025 — a **net-cash position of roughly US$ 21 billion**, or close to half of today's market cap ([Baidu 2025 Form 20-F, F-26 / p. 168](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1329099/000119312526109289/d83577d20f.htm)). EV/Sales is therefore closer to ~0.15–0.20× on a TTM basis, which is the cheapest multiple in any major-cap global AI company list.

## 2. COMPANY HISTORY

Baidu was founded in **January 2000** in Beijing by **Robin Yanhong Li (李彦宏)** and **Eric Yong Xu (徐勇)**. Li, then a 31-year-old engineer who had worked at Infoseek in Silicon Valley, returned to China with a search-relevance algorithm — RankDex (1996), one of the earliest hyperlink-based ranking systems and a near-contemporary of Google's PageRank — and a vision of a Chinese-language web index. The company name 百度 ("a hundred times") is drawn from a Song-dynasty poem by Xin Qiji and means a constant, repeated search ([Baidu corporate "About" page](https://home.baidu.com/about/index.html)). Eric Xu left in 2004; Li has remained chairman from inception and CEO since February 2004 ([Baidu 2025 Form 20-F, p. 170](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1329099/000119312526109289/d83577d20f.htm)).

The 2000s were a story of three things: scaling P4P search advertising (Baidu's monetisation model from 2001), forcing Google out of the China market (Google.cn ceased mainland operations in March 2010 after Baidu had already won >75% search share) and listing on NASDAQ in August 2005 in what was at the time the most successful US tech IPO since Google's, ending its first day up 354%. By 2010 Baidu was the dominant Chinese-language search engine with roughly 80% share and a market cap exceeding US$ 30 billion. The 2010s diversified the business: Baidu acquired the streaming-video startup Qiyi.com in November 2012 (later relaunched as iQIYI), launched its smartphone search-feed strategy, opened its first AI research lab in Sunnyvale, California in 2014, and hired former Microsoft AI head Qi Lu as Chief Operating Officer in 2017. iQIYI was spun out and listed separately on NASDAQ (IQ) in March 2018, with Baidu retaining majority control.

Two strategic shifts in the late 2010s would define Baidu today. The first was the **Apollo open autonomous-driving platform**, announced in April 2017 as an "Android of self-driving" — a stack the company would license to OEMs while building its own Apollo Go robotaxi service in parallel. By 2019 Apollo was conducting public road tests in Changsha; by November 2021 Apollo Go began charging passengers for autonomous rides in Beijing; by 2022 it received the first permits in China for fully driverless commercial rides in Chongqing and Wuhan ([Baidu 2025 Form 20-F, p. 121](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1329099/000119312526109289/d83577d20f.htm)). The second was **the ERNIE foundation-model line**, beginning with ERNIE 1.0 in 2019 (knowledge-enhanced pretraining for Chinese NLP) and culminating in **ERNIE Bot**, the first ChatGPT-class conversational AI launched in China, opened to the public on 16 March 2023 — five weeks before the second-place product from a Chinese competitor ([Reuters, 2023-08-31](https://www.reuters.com/technology/chinas-baidu-launches-erniebot-public-after-beijings-approval-2023-08-31/)). Baidu has aggressively iterated the model line since: ERNIE 3.5 (May 2023), ERNIE 4.0 (October 2023), ERNIE 4.0 Turbo (June 2024), ERNIE 4.5 and ERNIE X1 — its first reasoning model — in March 2025, ERNIE 4.5 Turbo / X1 Turbo in April 2025, and **ERNIE 5.0, a native omni-modal model**, in November 2025, with an updated version in January 2026 ([Baidu 2025 Form 20-F, p. 119–120](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1329099/000119312526109289/d83577d20f.htm)).

Major acquisitions and corporate moves shaped the perimeter: the US$ 1.9 billion 2014 acquisition of 91 Wireless (mobile app store); the 2017 sale of the Baidu Takeaway food-delivery unit to Ele.me; the 2020 listing of Smart Living Group (DuerOS / smart speakers) via a Series A round at RMB 20 billion post-money, followed by Series B rounds at US$ 5.1 billion in 2021 and 2022 ([Baidu 2025 Form 20-F, p. 117](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1329099/000119312526109289/d83577d20f.htm)); the August 2020 strategic acquisition of YY Live from JOYY for US$ 3.6 billion (live-streaming entertainment); and the August 2024 acquisition of a controlling stake in **JOYY's domestic Chinese livestreaming business** to extend feed-monetisation into shoppable video. In March 2021 Baidu completed a **dual primary listing in Hong Kong (9888.HK)**, raising approximately US$ 3.1 billion as a hedge against ADR delisting risk that was peaking that year ([HKEX listing prospectus, 2021-03](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2021/0309/2021030901046.pdf)).

The last three years have been defined by the AI transformation. The company has explicitly accepted near-term revenue and profit pain in the legacy search business to renovate it around generative-AI answer experiences. In 2024 Baidu redesigned Baidu Search to integrate ERNIE-generated answers above the traditional ten blue links, lowering ad inventory in the short term ([Baidu 2024 Form 20-F, p. 119](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1329099/000119312526109289/d83577d20f.htm)). In April 2025 ERNIE Bot was made **free to all users on PC and mobile** after eighteen months as a paid subscription, as inference costs collapsed ([Baidu 2025 Form 20-F, p. 124](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1329099/000119312526109289/d83577d20f.htm)). In June 2025 Baidu **open-sourced the ERNIE 4.5 series** (ten model variants of different sizes and architectures), a notable shift toward the open-weights camp that Meta, DeepSeek, Alibaba Qwen and Mistral now lead. The 4Q 2025 segment rename from "Baidu Core" to "Baidu General Business" signals that management wants to communicate the Core business as a single integrated AI-first revenue base rather than the historical "advertising + cloud + other" stack. In parallel, Apollo Go expanded from eight Chinese cities in early 2025 to a global footprint of 26 cities by February 2026, including international launches in Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Hong Kong, Switzerland (St. Gallen, with PostBus partnership), the UK (with Uber and Lyft pilots in London) and South Korea (Seoul metropolitan area) ([Baidu 2025 Form 20-F, p. 122](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1329099/000119312526109289/d83577d20f.htm)).

## 3. MANAGEMENT TEAM

### Robin Yanhong Li (李彦宏) — Co-founder, Chairman & Chief Executive Officer

Robin Li, 57, has run Baidu continuously since inception in January 2000, as chairman from day one and as CEO from February 2004 ([Baidu 2025 Form 20-F, p. 170](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1329099/000119312526109289/d83577d20f.htm)). Born in Yangquan, Shanxi province in 1968, Li graduated from Peking University with a bachelor's degree in information science (1991) and earned a master's in computer science from the State University of New York at Buffalo (1994). Before founding Baidu he worked at Dow Jones-owned IDD Information Services as a senior consultant, where in 1996 he developed **RankDex**, one of the earliest hyperlink-analysis search ranking algorithms — pre-dating Google's PageRank patent — and from 1997 to 1999 he served as a staff engineer at Infoseek, then a leading US web search engine ([Baidu corporate biography](https://home.baidu.com/about/management/robin-li-yanhong.html)). Li returned to China at the end of 1999 with US$ 1.2 million in Series A funding from DFJ and IDG and co-founded Baidu in a Resource Hotel room near Peking University.

Li's twenty-five-year tenure makes him one of the longest-serving founder-CEOs in global tech and gives him almost absolute strategic control. Through Handsome Reward Limited (a BVI vehicle wholly-owned by him) plus directly-held shares and proxies from employee-held shares, Li controls **59.9% of Baidu's voting power** thanks to the Class B (10-vote) super-voting share structure — even though his economic interest is approximately 16–18% ([Baidu 2025 Form 20-F, p. 178](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1329099/000119312526109289/d83577d20f.htm)). His record is mixed: he built and defended Baidu's mainland search monopoly against Google, but mistimed mobile (Tencent / WeChat captured the consumer-AI surface area), missed e-commerce (Alibaba), missed short-video (ByteDance / Douyin), and oversaw the **2016 Wei Zexi scandal** in which a young patient died after receiving experimental cancer treatment from a Baidu-advertised hospital, triggering a re-regulation of Chinese paid-search ([Reuters, 2016-05-09](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-baidu-china-investigation-idUSKCN0XY062/)). However, his correct early calls on autonomous driving (founding Apollo in 2013), open-source AI frameworks (PaddlePaddle, 2016) and pre-training foundation models (ERNIE 1.0, 2019) are now Baidu's three main asset bases. Li also serves on the board of New Oriental Education (NYSE: EDU). He is reportedly worth around US$ 4–5 billion.

### Haijian He (何海建) — Chief Financial Officer

Haijian He, 44, has been Baidu's CFO since **July 2025**, replacing Rong Luo, who held the position from 2021 to 2025 ([Baidu 2025 Form 20-F, p. 172](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1329099/000119312526109289/d83577d20f.htm)). He brings a rare combination of capital-markets pedigree and operating-CFO experience at a Chinese cloud business. He spent 2010 to 2015 at Bank of America Merrill Lynch and Citigroup Global Markets in equity capital markets; he then joined Goldman Sachs (Asia) L.L.C. as an executive director in the TMT and M&A groups from 2015 to early 2020; and from January 2020 to mid-2025 he was Executive Director and CFO of **Kingsoft Cloud Holdings Limited (NASDAQ: KC; HKEX: 3896)**, one of China's largest independent cloud-infrastructure providers, where he led the company's New York IPO in 2020 and Hong Kong dual listing in 2022 ([Kingsoft Cloud Form 20-F FY2023](https://www.sec.gov/cgi-bin/browse-edgar?action=getcompany&CIK=0001794846&type=20-F)). He holds bachelor's and master's degrees in electronic engineering from Southeast University, an MBA from the University of Chicago Booth School, completed the Harvard Business School Advanced Management Program, and is a CFA charterholder. In addition to the Baidu CFO role he serves as chairman of the board of iQIYI, Inc., reflecting Baidu's intent to centralise capital allocation across the parent and the listed subsidiary.

His appointment is widely read on the buy side as preparation for capital-markets actions — most plausibly a partial spin-out, REIT-style carve-out or strategic investment round at Apollo Go and/or Baidu AI Cloud — given his prior IPO and dual-listing leadership at Kingsoft Cloud ([Bloomberg, 2025-06-26](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-06-26/baidu-names-kingsoft-cloud-veteran-he-haijian-as-cfo)). His public commentary in the Q3 and Q4 2025 earnings calls emphasised disciplined opex (R&D fell 8% in 2025) and "monetising the AI capex cycle" rather than chasing scale-out share.

### Dou Shen (沈抖) — Executive Vice President, Baidu AI Cloud Group

Dou Shen, 47, is Baidu's most senior operating executive after Robin Li and runs both the **AI Cloud business** (Qianfan, ERNIE-as-a-service, infrastructure) and the **Mobile Ecosystem Group** (Baidu Search, Baidu App, Haokan, Baidu Wenku, Baidu Drive) — i.e., effectively all of Baidu Core / Baidu General Business apart from Intelligent Driving. He is not listed as a director on Baidu's 20-F (Baidu has a small five-member board) but he is the company's de facto Group President. Dou Shen joined Baidu in 2012 from Microsoft Bing, where he had worked on web search ranking, after earning a Ph.D. in computer science from the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology in 2007 and his bachelor's from Tianjin University. He rose through Baidu's search-ranking and feed teams, becoming Senior Vice President and head of the Mobile Ecosystem Group in 2020, and was promoted to Executive Vice President and head of Intelligent Cloud in May 2022 — the first time the cloud business had a dedicated EVP ([Baidu press release, 2022-05-05](https://ir.baidu.com/news-releases/news-release-details/baidu-announces-executive-promotions-and-management-changes)). In December 2023 he was given joint oversight of Mobile Ecosystem in addition to AI Cloud, consolidating Baidu's user-facing AI products under one executive. Dou Shen is widely credited with the cloud business's pivot from low-margin "private cloud" / smart-transportation system-integration projects (a drag through 2024) to the higher-margin Generative-AI MaaS revenue stream that drove cloud growth in 2025.

### Wang Yunpeng (王云鹏) — Senior Vice President, Intelligent Driving Group (Apollo Go)

Wang Yunpeng is Baidu's Senior Vice President leading the Intelligent Driving Group, which includes **Apollo Go (robotaxi), Apollo Self-Driving (consumer ADAS / NOA stack), and Apollo intelligent-transportation systems**. He took over from Zhenyu Li in 2022. Wang joined Baidu in 2014 and previously led the autonomous driving technology development team; he holds a Ph.D. in computer science from Tsinghua University. Under his leadership Apollo Go reached commercial fully-driverless operations across all eight mainland Chinese operating cities (Beijing, Shanghai, Shenzhen, Wuhan, Chengdu, Chongqing, Haikou and Sanya) by February 2025, crossed 20 million cumulative public rides by February 2026, and launched international operations in Hong Kong, the UAE, Switzerland, the UK and South Korea ([Baidu 2025 Form 20-F, p. 121–122](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1329099/000119312526109289/d83577d20f.htm); [Caixin Global, 2025-07-15](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2025-07-15/baidu-and-uber-team-up-to-deploy-thousands-of-robotaxis-globally-102233455.html)). The July 2025 multi-year partnership with **Uber Technologies** to deploy "thousands of Apollo Go autonomous vehicles" on the Uber platform across Asia and the Middle East, and the August 2025 deal with **Lyft** to deploy in Germany and the UK, are the most consequential externally-validating events for the unit since launch. Both are explicitly asset-light models, with Apollo Go providing the vehicles and the AI stack while Uber/Lyft handle dispatching and customer relationships — which closes the historical bull-case gap against Waymo, namely access to a global hailing demand network.

### Board composition and governance

Baidu's board of directors had five members as of the date of the FY2025 20-F: Robin Yanhong Li (executive chairman / CEO), and four independent directors — **Yuanqing Yang** (chairman and CEO of Lenovo Group, on the Baidu board since October 2015), **Jixun Foo** (Senior Managing Partner at Granite Asia / formerly GGV Capital, since July 2019), **Sandy Ran Xu** (CEO of JD.com, since January 2024) and **Xiaodan Liu** (Managing Partner of FirstLight Capital, audit-committee chair since February 2025) ([Baidu 2025 Form 20-F, p. 169–172](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1329099/000119312526109289/d83577d20f.htm)). Independent directors are a 4-of-5 majority, exceeding NASDAQ Foreign Private Issuer norms. **Insider ownership** is dominated by Robin Li: Class B super-voting shares plus proxies give him 59.9% of voting power, all directors and officers as a group control 59.9% (driven by Li), and the only other 5%-plus shareholder is **BlackRock, Inc.** with 4.7% economic / 1.6% voting ([Baidu 2025 Form 20-F, p. 180](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1329099/000119312526109289/d83577d20f.htm)). Aggregate executive cash compensation was a modest **RMB 12 million (US$ 2 million)** for FY2025, with 3,216,344 restricted Class A shares granted to executives — typical of Chinese-listed founder-controlled tech companies where the bulk of incentive comes through equity rather than cash.

## 4. PRODUCTS & SERVICES

Baidu's published product portfolio falls into five segments under Baidu General Business plus the iQIYI streaming business. The product enumeration below walks the company's own taxonomy from the FY2025 20-F (Item 4.B Business Overview) and the corporate site at [home.baidu.com](https://home.baidu.com/products/index.html).

**A. Mobile Ecosystem / Search-plus-Feed** (Baidu's largest revenue contributor, the legacy ad business)
- **Baidu App (百度App)** — flagship search-plus-feed mobile app, integrated with ERNIE-generated answers; **MAU 679 million in December 2025** ([Baidu 2025 Form 20-F, p. 128](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1329099/000119312526109289/d83577d20f.htm)). Pricing: P4P (cost-per-click) ad auction + display feed CPM. *Moat: yes — distribution moat from search-default status in China after Google's 2010 exit; data moat from indexed Chinese web. Closest competitor: Tencent WeChat Search (closed-ecosystem); ByteDance Toutiao / Douyin Search (open-feed). Status: at-parity-or-ahead on Chinese-language web search, behind on social-graph queries.*
- **ERNIE Bot (文心一言, rebranded "Wenxiaoyan" in some markets)** — conversational AI assistant, free since 1 April 2025. Powered by ERNIE 4.5 / 5.0. *Moat: partial — first-mover in China with regulatory approval (August 2023), but DeepSeek, Alibaba Qwen, ByteDance Doubao have closed the gap and Doubao is now the top consumer-AI app in China by MAU. Closest competitor: ByteDance Doubao. Status: behind on consumer mindshare, at-parity on benchmarks.*
- **Haokan (好看视频)** — short-video app embedded in the Mobile Ecosystem.
- **Baidu Post Bar (贴吧)** — long-running forum / interest-community product.
- **Baidu Knows (知道), Baidu Baike (百科), Baidu Map (地图), Baidu Translate, Baidu Input Method** — the long-tail of vertical and utility apps; "a portfolio of over one dozen apps" per the filing ([Baidu 2025 Form 20-F, p. 119](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1329099/000119312526109289/d83577d20f.htm)).
- **Baidu Wenku (文库) + Baidu Drive (网盘)** — consolidated into the **Personal Super Intelligence Business Group (PSIG)** in 2025; in August 2025 the two products jointly launched **GenFlow**, a multi-agent productivity platform ([Baidu 2025 Form 20-F, p. 128](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1329099/000119312526109289/d83577d20f.htm)). *Moat: partial — large installed file-storage base; Baidu Wenku is paid-subscription document/template marketplace.*

**B. AI Cloud (the second-largest and only growing revenue line in Baidu General Business)**
- **Qianfan (千帆) Model-as-a-Service Platform** — the central product of the AI Cloud, offering API access to ERNIE models plus third-party open-source models (DeepSeek, Llama, Qwen, etc.), training and fine-tuning toolkits, agent-orchestration runtime, and data services ([Baidu 2025 Form 20-F, p. 127](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1329099/000119312526109289/d83577d20f.htm)). *Moat: yes — proprietary ERNIE family + Kunlun chip integration + the largest Chinese-language model agent ecosystem outside Alibaba. Closest competitor: Alibaba Cloud Bailian / Tongyi (Qwen); Volcano Engine (ByteDance) for Doubao. Status: at parity on model performance, behind Alibaba and Volcano on cloud-IaaS share.*
- **ERNIE foundation models (open-sourced 4.5 series + closed 5.0)** — ERNIE 4.5 (March 2025), ERNIE X1 reasoning model (March 2025), ERNIE 4.5 Turbo / X1 Turbo (April 2025), ERNIE 5.0 omni-modal (November 2025), updated ERNIE 5.0 (January 2026). Ten model variants in the 4.5 open-weights release. *Moat: partial — open-sourcing partially commoditises the moat, but the closed-tier and inference-cost lead matter. Closest competitors: DeepSeek-V3 / R1 (open), Qwen 3 (open), Doubao (closed), Kimi K2 (closed). Status: at parity on Chinese-language benchmarks, modestly behind DeepSeek on math/code reasoning.*
- **Kunlun (昆仑) AI accelerator** — Baidu's in-house AI inference & training chip family, used internally for ERNIE inference and offered to enterprise customers as part of AI Cloud GPU-substitution stacks (key amid US export controls on Nvidia H100/H20). The third-generation Kunlun II is in mass deployment and a fourth-generation model was disclosed in 2025 ([Baidu Q3 2025 earnings call, 2025-11-18](https://ir.baidu.com/static-files/cd0c5b1b-4f3a-4ed3-9d8a-3ea3a8f0c2f5)). *Moat: yes (in a Chinese-domestic-only sense) — vertical integration with ERNIE; supports the export-controlled GPU substitution thesis. Competitor: Huawei Ascend 910B/910C, Cambricon, Iluvatar CoreX. Status: at parity within China, far behind Nvidia globally.*
- **PaddlePaddle (飞桨)** — open-source deep-learning framework, China's most-used local DL framework, anchoring the developer ecosystem.
- **Industry vertical cloud solutions** — financial-services AI cloud, manufacturing AI cloud, public-sector AI cloud, smart-transportation V2X solutions.

**C. Intelligent Driving & Other Growth Initiatives** — the segment that contains the headline "robotics brain" optionality.
- **Apollo Go (萝卜快跑, "Carrot Run")** — autonomous ride-hailing service. As of February 2026: 26 cities globally, 20+ million cumulative public rides, fully driverless operations in all 8 mainland Chinese cities, international pilots in Hong Kong, Abu Dhabi, Dubai, St. Gallen (Switzerland with PostBus), London (with Uber and Lyft) and Seoul ([Baidu 2025 Form 20-F, p. 121–122](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1329099/000119312526109289/d83577d20f.htm)). The **6th-generation Apollo Go vehicle**, RT6, is a purpose-built robotaxi built jointly with Geely / Jiyue, with bill-of-materials cost reported at under RMB 200,000 (US$ 28,000) per unit — well below Waymo's ~US$ 100k+ per vehicle ([Reuters, 2024-05-15](https://www.reuters.com/technology/baidu-launches-sixth-generation-driverless-robotaxi-2024-05-15/)). *Moat: yes — combination of regulatory permits (first-in-China for fully-driverless commercial in multiple cities), 20 million rides of driving data, in-house HD-mapping, and lowest per-vehicle cost globally. Closest competitor: Pony.ai (PONY, NASDAQ), WeRide (WRD), AutoX, Waymo (US). Status: ahead of Chinese peers on operational scale; at parity on tech with Waymo; ahead on per-vehicle unit economics.*
- **Apollo Self-Driving (ASD)** — consumer-grade L2++/L4 ADAS stack licensed to OEMs (Geely/Jiyue, BYD, GAC and others have used elements of the Apollo stack for highway-NOA and urban-NOA features). *Moat: partial — competing against the in-house stacks at XPeng, NIO, Tesla, Huawei ADS 3.0/4.0 and DJI Drive. Closest competitor: Huawei Intelligent Driving Solution (ADS 4.0).*
- **DuerOS / Xiaodu smart devices (小度)** — voice-assistant OS and Xiaodu-branded smart speakers, smart displays, smart screens, ed-tech (Xiaodu Learning Tablet) and smart-home products. Operated through Smart Living Group, a partially-owned subsidiary ([Baidu 2025 Form 20-F, p. 117](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1329099/000119312526109289/d83577d20f.htm)). *Moat: partial — entrenched in smart-speaker share but a commoditised consumer-hardware market. Closest competitor: Alibaba Tmall Genie; Xiaomi Xiao Ai.*
- **Robotics / embodied-AI partnerships** — Baidu has positioned itself as an **upstream "AI brain" provider** to humanoid-robotics OEMs rather than a robot maker itself. Public signals include the integration of ERNIE into UBTech's Walker-S humanoid robots (announced at Baidu World 2024 and reinforced at Baidu World 2025) and a strategic partnership with Tashan Robotics for embodied-AI agents ([UBTech press release, 2024-11-14](https://www.ubtrobot.com/en/about/news/details?id=193); [Baidu World 2025 keynote coverage, Caixin, 2025-11-13](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2025-11-13/baidu-unveils-ernie-50-omni-modal-model-and-deepens-humanoid-robotics-bets-102351772.html)). *Moat: forming. The omni-modal ERNIE 5.0 is positioned as a foundation model for embodied-AI use cases (vision-language-action). Closest competitor: Figure AI's Helix (US), Nvidia GR00T, ByteDance's robotics-LLM efforts, Unitree's in-house RL stack. Status: too early to declare leadership; positioning is credible because of the model + chip + cloud + autonomous-driving stack already proven at scale.*

**D. iQIYI (NASDAQ: IQ, controlled subsidiary)**
- **iQIYI streaming platform** — long-form video subscription + ad-supported tier; revenue RMB 27.3 billion in 2025, down 7% YoY ([Baidu 2025 Form 20-F, p. 148](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1329099/000119312526109289/d83577d20f.htm)).
- **iQIYI Pictures, iQIYI Sports, iQIYI Kids, iQIYI Lite, the metaverse-flavoured Mango / VR product set**.

**Flagship vs. long tail.** The three flagship products driving the equity narrative are: (1) **Baidu Search + Baidu App + ERNIE Bot** (still the revenue base, in transition); (2) **Apollo Go robotaxi** (the cleanest growth-options call); (3) **AI Cloud / Qianfan + Kunlun + ERNIE** (the bridge between today's earnings and the AI thesis). The long tail — Wenku/Drive/PSIG agents, DuerOS, the dozen-plus utility apps, iQIYI — together contribute meaningfully to revenue but command little equity-narrative premium. **Launches in the last 12 months**: ERNIE 4.5 (Mar 2025), ERNIE X1 (Mar 2025), ERNIE 4.5/X1 Turbo (Apr 2025), open-sourced ERNIE 4.5 series (Jun 2025), GenFlow multi-agent platform (Aug 2025), ERNIE 5.0 omni-modal (Nov 2025), updated ERNIE 5.0 (Jan 2026); Apollo Go international launches in UAE (Mar/Aug/Nov 2025), Hong Kong (Apr/Jun/Aug/Nov 2025, Feb 2026), Switzerland (Oct 2025), UK / South Korea (early 2026); Uber and Lyft partnerships (Jul/Aug 2025). **Sunsets**: ERNIE Bot subscription was retired (made free) in April 2025; legacy smart-transportation cloud-solution revenue continued to decline through 2024 ([Baidu 2025 Form 20-F, p. 148](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1329099/000119312526109289/d83577d20f.htm)).

## 5. CUSTOMERS & GO-TO-MARKET

Baidu's customer base differs sharply by segment.

**Online marketing** customers are highly **fragmented** — millions of Chinese SMEs and large enterprises bidding for keywords through the P4P auction system, plus third-party advertising agents that aggregate SME demand. Baidu explicitly identifies the third-party agents (not the end advertisers) as its customers of record: "We provide major sales incentives to third-party agents, which are identified as customers, that entitle them to receive price reduction on the online marketing services by meeting certain cumulative consumption requirements" ([Baidu 2025 Form 20-F, p. 153](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1329099/000119312526109289/d83577d20f.htm)). Contracts are not time-bound: "Our online marketing customers may choose to discontinue their business with us, which are not subject to fixed-term contracts" ([Baidu 2025 Form 20-F, p. 105](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1329099/000119312526109289/d83577d20f.htm)). Vertical mix has historically tilted toward healthcare, real estate, home furnishing and e-commerce, with healthcare materially curtailed since the 2016 Wei Zexi reforms.

**AI Cloud** customer base is mixed: large state-owned enterprises (SOEs) and ministries for smart-government and smart-transportation projects (historically the bulk of cloud revenue and the lowest-margin part of the book); large internet companies and unicorns consuming Qianfan API for generative AI use cases; banks, insurers and financial-services firms for ERNIE-powered chatbot and underwriting workflows; and a long tail of SMEs through self-service Qianfan API. Named enterprise customers disclosed by Baidu include **Geely Auto, China Mobile, China UnionPay, Industrial and Commercial Bank of China (ICBC), Bank of China, Ping An Insurance, Shanghai Pudong Development Bank, China Southern Power Grid, China Eastern Airlines, Postal Savings Bank of China, Yum China, Honor (smartphones), Samsung China, and Lenovo** ([Baidu AI Cloud customer references](https://cloud.baidu.com/customer-cases/) and Baidu World 2025 keynotes ([Caixin Global, 2025-11-13](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2025-11-13/baidu-unveils-ernie-50-omni-modal-and-deepens-humanoid-robotics-bets-102351772.html))).

**Apollo Go** customers are predominantly **B2C** (ride-hailing passengers in mainland China) but the international expansion has shifted to **B2B** platform partners — **Uber Technologies** (multi-year strategic partnership signed July 2025 to deploy "thousands of Apollo Go fully autonomous vehicles" on Uber across Asia / Middle East / UK / Dubai) and **Lyft, Inc.** (August 2025 deal for Germany and UK) ([Baidu 2025 Form 20-F, p. 122](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1329099/000119312526109289/d83577d20f.htm)). Strategic infrastructure partners include **PostBus** in Switzerland and **AutoGo** in Abu Dhabi.

**iQIYI** subscriber base is consumer; advertising customers are diversified across consumer brands.

**Customer concentration — quantified.** Baidu discloses in the FY2025 20-F that "**No customer generated greater than 10% of revenue during the years presented**" and that "As of December 31, 2025, the Group had no single customer with a balance exceeding 10% of the total accounts receivable" ([Baidu 2025 Form 20-F, F-27](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1329099/000119312526109289/d83577d20f.htm)). This applies to the consolidated revenue base in 2023, 2024 and 2025. Translated to the per-segment view: online marketing is structurally fragmented (no single advertiser approaches 1% of segment revenue), AI Cloud has named anchor accounts but none individually reaches the 10% threshold even within the segment (cloud revenue in 2025 was roughly RMB 25–28 billion estimated), and Apollo Go remains pre-material at the consolidated level. **There is therefore no top-1 or top-5 customer concentration risk to flag — the contrast with auto-Tier-1 suppliers (Mobileye / Innoviz / Hesai) and contract semiconductor businesses (TSMC) is stark.** The structural risk is instead **vertical concentration**: online marketing demand is sensitive to advertiser cyclicality in real estate, healthcare and consumer goods.

**Distribution and sales motion.** Baidu's go-to-market layers are: (a) the **Baidu Union** of third-party websites and apps that syndicate Baidu's ads in exchange for a revenue share — this is essentially Baidu's equivalent of Google AdSense and is responsible for a meaningful share of online marketing revenue, and traffic-acquisition costs to Baidu Union partners are a top-three line item in Baidu Core cost of revenue ([Baidu 2025 Form 20-F, p. 149](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1329099/000119312526109289/d83577d20f.htm)); (b) a **direct sales force** of approximately 6,400 people for large-account enterprise advertisers, cloud customers and key auto-OEM partners; (c) a **channel network of agencies** for SME advertisers; (d) **self-service Qianfan API** for cloud / model API consumption; (e) **Apollo Go's own consumer app** for ride-hailing demand, plus the Uber and Lyft platforms for international markets.

Customer case studies recently surfaced in Baidu's communications include: ICBC's ERNIE-powered intelligent advisory and credit-risk modelling deployment; Geely's use of Apollo for the Jiyue/Zeekr ADAS stack; China Eastern Airlines' use of Baidu Cloud for customer-service automation; and UBTech's integration of ERNIE multimodal models into the Walker-S humanoid robot's brain ([UBTech investor news, 2024-11-14](https://www.ubtrobot.com/en/about/news/details?id=193)).

## 6. INDUSTRY OVERVIEW

Baidu operates at the intersection of four large, fast-moving industries — and the equity narrative depends on getting the right weighting across all four.

**(1) Chinese digital advertising / online marketing.** The China digital advertising market was approximately **RMB 1.3 trillion (~US$ 180 billion)** in 2024 by aggregate spend, growing roughly 8–10% per year in nominal terms ([iResearch China Internet Ad Market Report 2025](https://www.iresearch.com.cn/Detail/report?id=4399&isfree=0)). Baidu's share, however, has been shrinking for over a decade. In 2014 Baidu Search captured roughly 30% of Chinese digital ad spend; by 2024 the company estimates put Baidu at sub-10%, with the gainers being ByteDance (Douyin / Toutiao / Ocean Engine, now ~30%+), Alibaba (Taobao + Alimama, ~20%), Tencent (WeChat ads + video accounts, ~12–15%), and Kuaishou (~8%) ([QuestMobile 2024 Annual China Mobile Internet Report](https://www.questmobile.com.cn/research/report/170)). The structural pressure is the migration from intent-based search advertising (Baidu's core auction) to interest-based feed and short-video advertising (Douyin's core) — a global pattern that has compressed Google's growth too, but more sharply in China because mobile traffic concentrates in app silos.

**(2) Generative-AI compute, model and application market.** China's generative-AI market is estimated by IDC at **US$ 35 billion in 2025** rising to **~US$ 200 billion by 2030** (CAGR ~40%), with the model-layer (foundation model APIs, MaaS) representing 15–20% of spend and the application + agent layer compounding faster ([IDC China Generative AI Forecast, 2025](https://www.idc.com/getdoc.jsp?containerId=prCHE52823025)). Inference compute is the largest line item. The structural question is who captures rents: chip vendors (Nvidia internationally, Huawei Ascend / Cambricon / Kunlun domestically), hyperscaler clouds (Alibaba Cloud, Volcano Engine, Huawei Cloud, Baidu Cloud, Tencent Cloud), foundation-model labs (DeepSeek, Moonshot/Kimi, Zhipu, Baichuan, MiniMax, plus the hyperscalers' in-house labs), or application/agent layers. Baidu is unusual in covering all four layers; the trade-off is execution focus.

**(3) Robotaxi / Mobility-as-a-Service (MaaS).** The global robotaxi market is a top-quartile growth call: McKinsey estimates a **US$ 100–400 billion revenue opportunity by 2030 and >US$ 1 trillion by 2040** in the aggregate Mobility-as-a-Service category, with autonomous ride-hailing capturing roughly half of that ([McKinsey Center for Future Mobility, "The future of mobility 2030", 2025-03](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/automotive-and-assembly/our-insights)). Today only two operators are at scale globally: **Waymo** (>10 million paid rides in the US through end-2025, primarily Phoenix / San Francisco / Los Angeles / Austin) and **Apollo Go** (>20 million cumulative public rides as of Feb 2026, primarily Chinese cities plus pilot markets). Pony.ai, WeRide and AutoX in China and Cruise (now wound down) / Zoox / Tesla Robotaxi in the US are the closest follow-ons. Penetration is sub-0.1% of total ride-hailing trips globally — the runway is the entire market.

**(4) Long-form online video.** iQIYI competes in a saturated Chinese long-form video market against Tencent Video, Youku (Alibaba) and Mango TV, with structural competition from short-video (Douyin / Kuaishou) and micro-drama platforms. The market is **roughly RMB 200 billion in 2024**, declining low-single-digit on subscription revenue and growing low-single-digit on ad revenue ([iResearch China Online Video Report 2024](https://www.iresearch.com.cn/Detail/report?id=4350&isfree=0)). It is the lowest-growth and lowest-multiple business in the Baidu portfolio.

**Regulatory environment.** Generative AI in China is governed by the **Interim Measures for the Management of Generative AI Services** (effective 15 August 2023), which require security assessment and algorithm filing before any public-facing GenAI service ([Cyberspace Administration of China — Interim Measures, 2023-07-13](http://www.cac.gov.cn/2023-07/13/c_1690898327029107.htm)). Baidu completed its filing before launching ERNIE Bot. Autonomous driving is regulated city-by-city via local permits — Baidu's permits in Beijing, Wuhan, Chongqing, Shenzhen, Shanghai, Hong Kong (testing) and Abu Dhabi (commercial) are explicitly cited in the FY2025 20-F ([Baidu 2025 Form 20-F, p. 121–122](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1329099/000119312526109289/d83577d20f.htm)). Data security is governed by the Cybersecurity Law (2017), Data Security Law (2021), Personal Information Protection Law (2021) and the Regulations on Network Data Security (effective January 2025). All of Baidu's main businesses are classified as "large network platforms" with enhanced data-protection obligations.

**Industry structure.** China internet is an oligopoly of five-to-seven players (BAT + ByteDance + JD + Meituan + Pinduoduo) operating largely walled-garden ecosystems. Generative AI is more fragmented but consolidating around a handful of foundation-model labs that can fund GPU spend (the hyperscalers + DeepSeek + Moonshot + Zhipu). Robotaxi is a two-horse race globally with a long tail. Streaming video is a three-horse race in China.

## 7. COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

Baidu names its own competitors explicitly in the FY2025 20-F: "internet companies, online marketing platforms in China, other search engines, players in Generative AI and foundation models, cloud services providers and robotaxi providers" ([Baidu 2025 Form 20-F, p. 106](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1329099/000119312526109289/d83577d20f.htm)). The competitor set is therefore segment-specific.

**Search-plus-feed advertising:**
- **ByteDance (private, "Doubao" / "Toutiao" / "Douyin Search")** — the most threatening competitor. ByteDance's Douyin Search has been gaining query share from Baidu Search since 2022 because Chinese mobile users increasingly initiate "search" inside Douyin's video feed; ByteDance disclosed >700 million Douyin DAU in 2024 ([ByteDance corporate site](https://www.bytedance.com/en/products); [TechNode, 2024-05](https://technode.com/2024/05/16/douyin-claims-700-million-daily-active-users/)).
- **Tencent (0700.HK, WeChat Search / Sogou)** — Tencent acquired Sogou in 2021 and integrated it into WeChat Search; the WeChat / Mini Program ecosystem captures intent-search inside the messenger.
- **Alibaba (BABA, Quark)** — Alibaba's Quark browser-plus-search app pivoted aggressively to AI-answer in 2024–2025 and has grown rapidly, though from a small base.
- **Microsoft Bing (China)** — small share but the only foreign search engine to remain in mainland China.

**Generative AI / foundation models:**
- **Alibaba Cloud (BABA, "Qwen" / Tongyi / Bailian MaaS)** — strongest hyperscaler-plus-model competitor; Qwen is the dominant open-weights Chinese model family by global Hugging Face downloads.
- **ByteDance / Volcano Engine ("Doubao" foundation model)** — fast-growing model API business with aggressive token-pricing (Doubao Pro is among the cheapest tokens in China); Doubao consumer app overtook ERNIE Bot in MAU in mid-2024.
- **DeepSeek (private, Hangzhou)** — disruptive open-source models (DeepSeek-V3 December 2024, DeepSeek-R1 January 2025) priced at ~3% of OpenAI's o1 rates; forced industry-wide token-price cuts in early 2025 ([Reuters, 2025-01-27](https://www.reuters.com/technology/artificial-intelligence/what-is-deepseek-why-is-it-disrupting-ai-sector-2025-01-27/)).
- **Moonshot AI ("Kimi", private)**, **Zhipu AI ("GLM", private)**, **Baichuan**, **MiniMax**, **01.AI ("Yi", Kai-Fu Lee)** — the "AI Tiger" cohort.
- **Tencent ("Hunyuan", 0700.HK)** — increasingly integrated into Tencent Cloud + WeChat.
- **Huawei ("Pangu", with Ascend NPU stack)** — the closest direct competitor on full-stack AI (chip + framework + model + cloud), with stronger SOE / public-sector reach.

**AI Cloud / IaaS:** Alibaba Cloud (~38% China public cloud share), Huawei Cloud (~18%), Tencent Cloud (~15%), Baidu AI Cloud (~7–8%), China Telecom Cloud / China Mobile Cloud / China Unicom Cloud (collectively ~15%) per Canalys 2024 ([Canalys China Cloud Channels Analysis, 2024-Q4](https://www.canalys.com/insights/china-cloud-channels-analysis-q4-2024)).

**Robotaxi / autonomous driving:**
- **Waymo (Alphabet GOOGL subsidiary)** — global tech leader, US-only operations, 250k+ paid rides/week as of late 2025.
- **Pony.ai (PONY, NASDAQ; HKEX)** — China robotaxi peer, listed in November 2024 at US$ 13 IPO price.
- **WeRide (WRD, NASDAQ)** — China robotaxi peer, also listed 2024.
- **AutoX (private)** — China robotaxi.
- **Tesla (TSLA, Robotaxi unsupervised FSD)** — entering robotaxi in 2025–2026 with Cybercab.
- **Cruise (GM)** — wound down 2024.
- **Zoox (Amazon)** — commercial launch 2025.

**Smart speakers / voice assistants:** Alibaba Tmall Genie, Xiaomi Xiao Ai. Baidu's Xiaodu had peaked at ~25% global smart-speaker share by units in 2020 (per Strategy Analytics) but the segment has commoditised.

**Streaming video:** Tencent Video, Youku (Alibaba), Mango TV, plus Douyin and Kuaishou for short-video substitution.

**Positioning framework.** On the dimension of (i) **price** — Baidu is at parity with peers on Qianfan token pricing after the early-2025 cuts forced by DeepSeek and Volcano Engine. On (ii) **features / model capability** — Baidu's ERNIE 4.5 / 5.0 are at parity with Alibaba Qwen 3 and modestly behind DeepSeek-R1 on math/code reasoning, ahead on multimodal omni-modal generation (per third-party benchmarks at OpenCompass). On (iii) **scale** — Baidu is behind Alibaba and ByteDance on cloud installed base and DAU mindshare for consumer AI, ahead of all Chinese peers on autonomous-driving operational scale (20 million Apollo Go rides vs. low single-digit millions for Pony.ai / WeRide).

**Competitive advantages:** (a) **Full-stack vertical integration** from chip (Kunlun) to framework (PaddlePaddle) to model (ERNIE) to cloud (Qianfan) to applications (Search + Maps + Apollo) is unique within China; only Huawei and partially Alibaba have a comparable stack. (b) **Regulatory and operational head-start in Chinese robotaxi** — first license for fully-driverless commercial rides in multiple cities, 20+ million rides of safety record, Uber/Lyft global partnerships. (c) **Lowest-cost robotaxi platform globally** — RT6 BOM is reportedly below US$ 28k. (d) **The largest Chinese-language search index and the largest legacy enterprise content / customer-service deployment base for AI integration**.

**Competitive vulnerabilities:** (a) **Decaying search-ad monetization** — secular trend, no obvious reversal. (b) **Lost consumer-AI mindshare to ByteDance Doubao and DeepSeek**, both of which are growing MAU faster than ERNIE Bot. (c) **Sub-scale cloud business** — Baidu Cloud has roughly half the share of Tencent Cloud and one-fifth of Alibaba Cloud, sub-scale relative to fixed-cost AI capex. (d) **Geopolitical exposure** — US export controls on H100/H200/B100 GPUs constrain training scale for ERNIE 6+; Baidu has substituted with Kunlun but the parity gap with Nvidia stacks is real. (e) **Concentrated equity structure** — Robin Li controls 59.9% voting, limiting hostile change-of-control catalysts.

## 8. MARKET OPPORTUNITY (TAM)

We size the opportunity by Baidu's three growth pillars rather than the entirety of Chinese internet (which would inflate TAM artificially since Baidu has lost share in the legacy categories).

**(A) Generative-AI MaaS + foundation-model API revenue (Qianfan + ERNIE).** China generative-AI total addressable market (TAM) is estimated by IDC at **US$ 35 billion in 2025** scaling to **~US$ 200 billion by 2030** (~40% CAGR) ([IDC China Generative AI Forecast, 2025](https://www.idc.com/getdoc.jsp?containerId=prCHE52823025)). The model-API / MaaS slice is ~15–20% of that — call it **SAM ~US$ 30–40 billion by 2030**. If Baidu captures 12–18% of that slice — its current Qianfan share is roughly in that band per Canalys — implied **SOM is US$ 4–7 billion by 2030**, vs. an estimated Baidu cloud-services revenue of US$ 4 billion in 2025 (i.e., a near-doubling on the model/AI line item alone, on top of existing IaaS revenue).

**(B) Robotaxi / autonomous ride-hailing (Apollo Go).** Global robotaxi TAM by 2030 is in the US$ 100–400 billion range per McKinsey, and the China robotaxi sub-market alone is estimated at **US$ 50–100 billion by 2030** by Goldman Sachs ([Goldman Sachs Research, "China Autonomous Driving — The Robotaxi Tipping Point", 2024-09](https://www.goldmansachs.com/intelligence/pages/china-robotaxi-2024.html)). If Apollo Go captures **20–30% of the China robotaxi market** (it has 50%+ of mainland Chinese robotaxi rides today), 2030 China-domestic revenue is **US$ 10–30 billion**. International scale via Uber and Lyft adds optionality — even a 5% global share outside China at maturity is another US$ 5–10 billion. Per-trip economics: Apollo Go has not yet broken out unit profitability, but third-party teardown of the RT6 with a ~5-year vehicle life suggests breakeven on roughly RMB 1.6 per kilometer at 70% utilisation, well below incumbent ride-hailing operator cost ([Caixin Global, 2024-08-08](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2024-08-08/wuhan-residents-share-thoughts-on-baidus-driverless-taxis-as-popularity-grows-102219893.html)).

**(C) Embodied-AI / humanoid robotics "brain" layer.** Goldman Sachs sized the global humanoid-robot market at **US$ 38 billion by 2035** (base case) to **US$ 154 billion** (blue sky) ([Goldman Sachs Research, "Humanoid Robots, The Trillion-Dollar Opportunity", 2024-01-08](https://www.goldmansachs.com/intelligence/pages/the-trillion-dollar-opportunity-for-humanoid-robots.html)). Within that, the "AI brain" software-and-cloud layer — analogous to mobile-OS economics — could capture 10–15% of system economics, so a **SAM of US$ 4–20 billion by 2035** for foundation-model and cloud providers serving humanoid OEMs. Baidu is one of a handful of Chinese providers (with Huawei, Alibaba and ByteDance) credibly positioned at the omni-modal model layer. Realistic SOM: 10–20% of the Chinese share of that pie, so **US$ 0.5–4 billion by 2035** at maturity — small in absolute terms but valuable for multiple expansion.

**Penetration strategy.** Baidu's near-term wedge is "the only Chinese full-stack AI company that has a deployed robotaxi at global scale" — a positioning that ports cleanly into the embodied-AI narrative because perception, planning and decision-making on a humanoid robot is closely related to perception, planning and decision-making in a robotaxi. The integration with UBTech's Walker-S humanoid is the proof of concept; the Geely / Jiyue / Zeekr partnership is the proof for automotive. Internationally, Baidu has shifted to asset-light models (Apollo Go vehicles + Uber/Lyft dispatch) to avoid the capex trap that bankrupted Cruise. The strategy on the cloud / model side is **price-aggressive + ecosystem-aggressive** — Qianfan APIs cut prices roughly 80–95% versus 2023 to track DeepSeek and Volcano Engine, while open-sourcing ERNIE 4.5 to seed developer mindshare and pull the closed-tier ERNIE 5.0 into enterprise deals.

## 9. RISK ASSESSMENT

### Company-Specific Risks

**1. Online marketing structural decline.** Baidu's largest revenue stream — online marketing — is in secular decline because (i) AI-generated answers in Baidu Search cannibalise blue-link ad inventory, (ii) ByteDance / Douyin Search captures intent-query share, and (iii) macro pressure compresses healthcare/real-estate ad budgets. Online marketing revenue declined in 2024 and 2025; total revenue fell 3% in 2025 ([Baidu 2025 Form 20-F, p. 147](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1329099/000119312526109289/d83577d20f.htm)). Mitigant: AI Cloud is growing fast enough to roughly offset, but the cross-over to net growth is not yet evident.

**2. Execution risk on Apollo Go international rollout.** The Uber and Lyft partnerships are the highest-stakes catalyst, but they require regulatory approval in dozens of new jurisdictions, vehicle certification, insurance underwriting and operations capability that Baidu has never run outside China. Even one high-profile fatality could pause global rollout, as Cruise saw in 2023. Mitigant: asset-light model limits capex exposure; Uber and Lyft carry brand risk too and have selected Apollo Go after due diligence.

**3. Key-person dependency on Robin Li.** Li, 57, is co-founder, CEO, chairman and controlling shareholder with 59.9% voting power. There is no public succession plan, and the board has only one executive director. A health event or step-down would create operational and governance uncertainty. Mitigant: Dou Shen and Wang Yunpeng have operational depth; the board has four independent directors.

**4. Consumer-AI mindshare loss.** ERNIE Bot was the first Chinese ChatGPT competitor in March 2023 but has been overtaken by ByteDance Doubao on consumer MAU and by DeepSeek on developer mindshare. If the consumer-AI race is winner-take-most, Baidu may have to settle for the enterprise-AI segment, which has lower multiples. Mitigant: open-sourcing ERNIE 4.5 to rebuild developer mindshare; integration of ERNIE into Baidu App (679m MAU).

**5. Long-lived asset impairment recurrence.** The 2025 RMB 16.2 billion (US$ 2.3 billion) impairment of the Core asset group ([Baidu 2025 Form 20-F, p. 147](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1329099/000119312526109289/d83577d20f.htm)) signals that capex into AI infrastructure may not generate the returns originally modelled. Goodwill on the balance sheet is RMB 36.8 billion (US$ 5.3 billion); a further impairment is possible if cloud growth disappoints. Mitigant: still no goodwill impairment as of FY2025; impairment was a non-cash charge.

**6. iQIYI structural drag.** iQIYI revenue declined 7% in 2025 to RMB 27.3 billion, with content competition from micro-drama and short-video. iQIYI had a working capital deficit at year-end 2025, and Baidu's consolidated debt includes RMB 14.8 billion of iQIYI obligations ([Baidu 2025 Form 20-F, p. 91 and p. 95](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1329099/000119312526109289/d83577d20f.htm)). Mitigant: iQIYI is separately listed (IQ); a partial or full deconsolidation is plausible.

### Industry / Market Risks

**7. Competitive intensity in foundation models and cloud.** Token-price compression in 2024–2025 (DeepSeek, Volcano Engine, Tencent Hunyuan) shrinks gross margin per query for all Chinese MaaS providers. ERNIE Bot subscription was eliminated in April 2025 ([Baidu 2025 Form 20-F, p. 124](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1329099/000119312526109289/d83577d20f.htm)) — direct revenue loss. Mitigant: lower inference costs from Kunlun chips improve unit economics.

**8. US export controls on AI chips.** US restrictions on Nvidia H100/H200/B100/H20 limit Baidu's training-compute scaling; substitution to Kunlun and Huawei Ascend works at parity for inference but is materially slower at frontier training scale. Mitigant: domestic chip ecosystem (SMIC + Huawei + Kunlun) is improving; ERNIE 4.5 was trained on a mix of stockpiled Nvidia + Kunlun + Ascend.

**9. Regulatory tightening on generative AI.** China's Interim Measures (August 2023), the Network Data Security Regulations (effective January 2025) and the Draft Measures for Large Online Platforms (November 2025) all increase compliance overhead. Any future content-safety incident could trigger temporary product suspension. Mitigant: Baidu completed the security assessment ahead of ERNIE Bot launch and has a long track record of operating in the Chinese regulatory environment.

**10. Robotaxi safety incident risk.** Any high-profile accident causing injury or death involving an Apollo Go vehicle — domestically or internationally — could trigger permit suspension, a public-trust collapse, or both. Mitigant: 20 million rides accumulated without a major reported casualty; the per-mile safety record exceeds Chinese human-driver benchmarks.

### Financial Risks

**11. ADR-delisting / VIE structural risk.** Baidu derives 50% of external revenue through VIEs in mainland China ([Baidu 2025 Form 20-F, p. 132](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1329099/000119312526109289/d83577d20f.htm)). The HFCAA and PCAOB-inspection regime created delisting risk that peaked in 2022; the company's 2021 Hong Kong dual primary listing is the principal mitigant. A re-escalation in US-China financial decoupling could re-introduce this risk.

**12. Sum-of-the-parts discount (valuation / multiple-compression-risk).** TTM P/E is 78× (skewed by 2025 impairment); ex-impairment is ~16×. P/S is 0.36×, EV/S ~0.15–0.20×, both at significant discount to global peers (Alphabet 11.4× P/S, Microsoft 9.8× P/S, Tencent 5.4× P/S). The market is pricing BIDU as a stagnating ad business and assigning zero value to Apollo Go and AI Cloud. Re-rating requires either explicit segment-level disclosure for AI Cloud and Apollo Go, a partial spin-out of one of those units, or sustained revenue acceleration. **The risk is not multiple-compression but multiple persistence** — a stretched timeline where the embedded options stay embedded indefinitely.

### Macroeconomic Risks

**13. China consumer / advertiser macro.** Baidu's online marketing revenue is highly cyclical to Chinese consumer-goods, real-estate and healthcare advertiser budgets, all of which were weak in 2024 and 2025 ([Baidu 2025 Form 20-F, p. 105–106](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1329099/000119312526109289/d83577d20f.htm)). A continued property-sector slump would extend the advertising drag.

**14. RMB / USD FX exposure.** RMB depreciated approximately 4.19% against the USD in 2025 ([Baidu 2025 Form 20-F, F-27](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1329099/000119312526109289/d83577d20f.htm)). Substantially all revenue is RMB-denominated; about US$ 12.8 billion of debt is USD-denominated. A further 10% RMB depreciation would create translation losses and increase the RMB cost of servicing USD debt by RMB 9 billion.

**15. Geopolitical and trade-policy risk.** Apollo Go's international expansion (Uber, Lyft, UAE, UK, South Korea, Switzerland) is exposed to escalation of US–China technology restrictions, export controls on autonomous-driving software, and EU-China trade frictions. Mitigant: the asset-light partnership model with local platform operators (Uber, Lyft, PostBus) creates political insulation.
