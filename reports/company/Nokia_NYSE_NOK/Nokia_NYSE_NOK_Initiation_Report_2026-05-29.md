## Investment Summary

We initiate coverage of **Nokia Corporation (NYSE: NOK, Helsinki: NOKIA)** with a **HOLD** rating and a **12-month price target of USD 18.50**, implying +12.4% upside from the May 27, 2026 close of USD 16.46. Nokia has executed a credible strategic transformation under new CEO Justin Hotard — Infinera close on 28 Feb 2025, NVIDIA strategic investment on 28 Oct 2025, and the 19 Nov 2025 Capital Markets Day reshape into two operating segments effective 1 Jan 2026 — and Q1 2026 results validated the trajectory with AI & Cloud customer revenue +49% YoY and Network Infrastructure (NI) 2026 growth guide raised to 12–14% ([Nokia Q1 2026 interim PDF](https://www.nokia.com/system/files/2026-04/nokia_results_2026_q1.pdf)). However, the stock has roughly quadrupled in twelve months and now trades at ~4.2× FY2025 sales and ~42× comparable operating profit — multiples that already discount substantial Bull-case execution. Our HOLD reflects fair-to-fully valued at current levels rather than a fundamental concern about the business pivot.

## Investment Thesis

### Pillar 1: Network Infrastructure is now the AI-data-fabric story

Network Infrastructure (NI) reached EUR 7,986 million in FY2025 net sales — 40% of group and the largest single segment after the Infinera consolidation. The 20-F's sub-segment disclosure (verbatim from p. 72): *"In 2025, net sales include Optical Networks net sales of EUR 3,019 million, IP Networks net sales of EUR 2,594 million and Fixed Networks net sales of EUR 2,373 million"* ([Form 20-F p. 72](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/924613/000162828026015034/nok-20251231.htm)). On a stand-alone basis NI now contains the highest-quality elements of the group — proprietary optical-DSP silicon (PSE-6s + Infinera ICE7), in-house indium phosphide PIC manufacturing (Sunnyvale, CA), the FP5 IP routing silicon, and #1-globally xPON OLT leadership for the sixth consecutive year ([Nokia 20-F p. 22](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/924613/000162828026015034/nok-20251231.htm)).

The Q1 2026 print validated the trajectory: Optical Networks +20% YoY, AI & Cloud customers +49% YoY, EUR 1 billion of Q1 orders from AI & Cloud customers, and management raised the FY2026 NI growth guide from the CMD framework's 6–8% CAGR setup to **12–14% for the full year** ([Q1 2026 PDF](https://www.nokia.com/system/files/2026-04/nokia_results_2026_q1.pdf); [Investing.com Q1 2026 summary](https://www.investing.com/news/company-news/nokia-q1-2026-slides-ai-boom-drives-guidance-raise-margins-expand-93CH-4631645)).

### Pillar 2: NVIDIA strategic partnership creates a credible AI-RAN platform option

On 28 October 2025, NVIDIA announced a USD 1.0 billion equity investment in newly issued Nokia ordinary shares at USD 6.01/share (~2.9% pro-forma stake) plus a multi-year AI-RAN collaboration built around NVIDIA's Aerial RAN Computer Pro (ARC-Pro) — a 6G-ready GPU-accelerated baseband for base-of-tower deployment, with T-Mobile US as launch carrier, field trials starting early 2026, and commercial GA late 2027 ([NVIDIA investor release](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2025/NVIDIA-and-Nokia-to-Pioneer-the-AI-Platform-for-6G--Powering-Americas-Return-to-Telecommunications-Leadership/default.aspx); [Nokia 6-K filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000924613/000110465925103038/tm2529638d1_6k.htm)). The 20-F describes the integration: *"we leverage the NVIDIA ARC-Pro accelerated computing platform to evolve our Cloud RAN into a Cloud AI-RAN solution, and in our AirScale RAN solutions"* ([Form 20-F p. 17](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/924613/000162828026015034/nok-20251231.htm)).

The NVIDIA partnership matters in two ways. First, it gives Nokia a differentiated AI-RAN product option that neither Ericsson nor Samsung Networks can replicate at scale — an important strategic moat for the next RAN refresh cycle. Second, it creates a credible data-center networking angle: NVIDIA's investment was paired with optical and IP switching collaboration, opening a potential design-win path into NVIDIA's AI infrastructure architecture ([Frank Rayal — NVIDIA/Nokia analysis](https://frankrayal.com/2025/11/03/nvidia-and-nokia-a-strategic-bet-on-edge-ai-ran/)).

### Pillar 3: Patent licensing is a stable EUR 1.4B annuity with optionality

Nokia Technologies (NT) generated EUR 1,501 million in FY2025 net sales at 70.6% operating margin — making it the most profitable segment by margin in the group ([Form 20-F p. 73](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/924613/000162828026015034/nok-20251231.htm)). The patent portfolio comprises *"more than 26,000 families, including over 8,000 essential to 5G"* and 250+ active licensees including Apple, Samsung, Huawei, Xiaomi, OPPO, vivo, Honor, Lenovo, Mercedes-Benz, and Amazon (added March 2025 in a video-streaming settlement) ([Form 20-F pp. 13, 24, 184](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/924613/000162828026015034/nok-20251231.htm)). The smartphone renewal cycle was *"declared complete in February 2024"* — the major OEM contracts are locked in for 5–10 years, making the EUR 1.4 billion run-rate (per CEO Hotard, p. 7) a high-confidence annuity through 2028+ ([Nokia smartphone licensing release Feb 2024](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2024/02/08/2826094/0/en/Inside-information-Nokia-concludes-smartphone-patent-license-renewal-cycle.html)).

Expansion programs in automotive, consumer electronics, IoT and multimedia are now contributing *"an annual revenue run-rate of more than EUR 200 million"* with pending video-codec enforcement against Acer, Asus and Hisense in Germany — providing upside optionality beyond the smartphone base ([Form 20-F p. 13](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/924613/000162828026015034/nok-20251231.htm)).

### Pillar 4: Fortress balance sheet enables capital returns through any cycle

FY2025 ended with EUR 6,791 million in total cash & IB investments, EUR 3,413 million in IB liabilities, and a **net cash position of EUR 3,378 million** ([Form 20-F p. 66](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/924613/000162828026015034/nok-20251231.htm)). Order backlog of EUR 19.5 billion provides ~12 months of forward revenue visibility. Capital return has been steady: **EUR 2.1 billion of dividends paid since 2023 and EUR 1.6 billion of share buybacks**, with the FY2025 proposed dividend of EUR 0.14/share maintained ([Form 20-F p. 10](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/924613/000162828026015034/nok-20251231.htm)). At our base-case projections, Nokia can absorb cumulative FY2026E–FY2030E dividends + buybacks of ~EUR 10 billion while still ending FY2030 with net cash of EUR 8 billion+ ([financial model — Balance Sheet tab](model/Nokia_NYSE_NOK_Financial_Model_2026-05-29.xlsx)).

### Pillar 5: Mix shift toward higher-multiple segments justifies SOME premium re-rating

The structural mix shift is real. In FY2023, Network Infrastructure was 30% of group; in FY2025 it is 40%; in our FY2030E base case it reaches 45%. Mobile Networks fell from 39% to 39% to 31% over the same window. The shift moves Nokia closer to the Cisco/Arista/Ciena valuation bucket and further from the Ericsson/ZTE bucket. *Analyst view:* a modest premium to the historical 1.0–1.5× EV/Sales is defensible; the question is whether the ~4.2× currently embedded is too aggressive.

## Key Risks to Our Thesis

We identify twelve principal risks distributed across four categories. Detailed quantification appears in the Risk Assessment section of the linked research document ([Nokia Research Document §9](Nokia_NYSE_NOK_Research_Document.md)).

**Strategy & execution risks.** (1) Infinera integration cost overruns — EUR 646 M customer-relationship intangibles and EUR 380 M technology intangibles must amortize through 2028; integration synergies (DSP roadmap consolidation between PSE-6s and ICE7) are non-trivial ([Form 20-F Note 6.2 p. 185](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/924613/000162828026015034/nok-20251231.htm)). (2) AI-RAN commercialization slip — late 2027 GA leaves 2026–27 P&L benefit limited to development and trial revenue ([NVIDIA partnership release](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2025/NVIDIA-and-Nokia-to-Pioneer-the-AI-Platform-for-6G--Powering-Americas-Return-to-Telecommunications-Leadership/default.aspx)). (3) Operating-model execution — collapsing five business groups into two effective 1 Jan 2026 is the largest organisational change since the Alcatel-Lucent integration ([Capacity Media — biggest overhaul in years](https://capacityglobal.com/news/nokia-announces-biggest-organisational-overhaul-in-years/)).

**Industry / market risks.** (4) Mobile RAN structural stagnation — Dell'Oro forecasts ~1% CAGR over the next five years ([Dell'Oro — RAN stabilized in 2025](https://www.delloro.com/news/ran-market-stabilized-in-2025/)). (5) Hyperscaler customer concentration — although no single customer exceeds 10% of group, the AI & Cloud + MCE&D bucket (15.5% of FY2025 sales) is concentrated across a handful of hyperscalers ([Form 20-F Note 2.1 p. 139](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/924613/000162828026015034/nok-20251231.htm)). (6) AT&T RAN runoff — Ericsson's December 2023 USD 14 billion Open RAN win continues to displace Nokia's wireless footprint at AT&T through 2028 ([AT&T release Dec 2023](https://about.att.com/story/2023/commercial-scale-open-radio-access-network.html)).

**Financial risks.** (7) Valuation multiple compression — at ~42× FY2025 comparable operating profit, NOK trades 2 standard deviations above its 2022–24 range; a reversion to 18–22× could produce 40–60% drawdown ([Yahoo Finance — NOK](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/NOK/)). (8) FX exposure to USD weakness — ~35% of sales are in the Americas region, with management already flagging "weaker dollar and US tariffs" as profitability drag ([Light Reading — Nokia tariff impact](https://www.lightreading.com/finance/nokia-profit-outlook-hurt-by-weaker-dollar-and-us-tariffs)). (9) Patent-licensing revenue lumpiness — the EUR 400 M+ FY2024 catch-up payment drove FY2025 NT -22% YoY; 5–10 year renewal cycles create quarterly volatility ([Form 20-F p. 75](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/924613/000162828026015034/nok-20251231.htm)).

**Geopolitical / regulatory risks.** (10) US tariffs (Trump-2.0 regime) — Nokia disclosed a ~USD 22–34 M Q2'25 tariff hit to comparable operating profit; CEO Hotard publicly said he would "absolutely entertain" expanding US manufacturing ([Fox Business — Hotard on US expansion](https://www.foxbusiness.com/politics/nokia-exec-open-expanding-us-manufacturing-avoid-trump-tariffs)). (11) China decoupling — Greater China sales of EUR 913 M (4.6% of group) declining since 2022 cybersecurity-law tightening ([Form 20-F p. 96](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/924613/000162828026015034/nok-20251231.htm)). (12) Export-control evolution — NVIDIA's GPU export restrictions to China shape which AI-RAN products Nokia can sell into restricted markets ([Form 20-F risk factors p. 95](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/924613/000162828026015034/nok-20251231.htm)).

## Company 101

### Overview & history

Nokia Corporation traces to **12 May 1865**, when mining engineer **Fredrik Idestam** secured a Finnish-senate concession to establish a groundwood pulp mill at the Tammerkoski rapids in Tampere — then under the Russian Grand Duchy of Finland ([Wikipedia — Fredrik Idestam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fredrik_Idestam)). The modern conglomerate took shape through three legacy lines — pulp/paper (1865), rubber (Eduard Polón's Finnish Rubber Works, 1898) and cables (Finnish Cable Works, 1912) — which **merged into the present Nokia Corporation in 1967** ([Wikipedia — History of Nokia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Nokia)). 2025 marked Nokia's 160th anniversary and the 100th anniversary of Nokia Bell Labs — a coincidence the 2025 Annual Report leans on heavily ([Form 20-F p. 6](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/924613/000162828026015034/nok-20251231.htm)).

![Nokia history timeline](charts/chart_05_history_timeline.png)
*Figure 5: Nokia historical milestones, 1865–2026. The 2014 Microsoft devices sale and 2016 Alcatel-Lucent acquisition bracket the modern telecom-equipment business. (External source: [Wikipedia — History of Nokia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Nokia); [Form 20-F p. 6](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/924613/000162828026015034/nok-20251231.htm))*

The handset era ended decisively with the **April 2014 sale of Nokia Devices & Services to Microsoft for ~USD 7.2 billion** — the 20-F still carries tax-indemnity references to that disposal ([Form 20-F tax indemnity ~p. 108](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/924613/000162828026015034/nok-20251231.htm)). The **January 2016 EUR 15.6 billion all-share Alcatel-Lucent acquisition** transformed Nokia into a full-stack telecom-network vendor; the ALU brand was retired 3 November 2016 ([Form 20-F glossary p. 193](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/924613/000162828026015034/nok-20251231.htm)). The 2022–25 period brought further strategic shifts: Russia exit (April 2022), buyout of China Huaxin's ~50% stake in Nokia Shanghai Bell (Q4 2024), the Alcatel Submarine Networks sale to the French State (31 Dec 2024), and the Infinera close (28 Feb 2025) ([Form 20-F p. 71](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/924613/000162828026015034/nok-20251231.htm)).

### Management

**Justin Hotard — President & CEO since 1 April 2025.** Hotard joined directly from **Intel**, where he served as EVP and General Manager of the Data Center & AI Group (DCAI), Intel's server-CPU and AI-accelerator business ([Nokia leadership page — Justin Hotard](https://www.nokia.com/we-are-nokia/leadership-and-governance/group-leadership-team/justin-hotard/)). Prior to Intel, Hotard spent **nine years at Hewlett Packard Enterprise (2015–2024)**, finishing as EVP and GM of HPC, AI & Labs, where per Nokia's published bio *"he delivered the world's first exascale supercomputer for the US Department of Energy"* (Frontier at Oak Ridge) ([Nokia bio](https://www.nokia.com/we-are-nokia/leadership-and-governance/group-leadership-team/justin-hotard/)). Earlier career at NCR Corporation, Symbol Technologies, and Motorola (as an engineer deploying mobile networks for US carriers — the sole conventional telecom-vendor tour). Education: BSc Electrical Engineering, University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign; MBA, Northwestern Kellogg ([Nokia bio](https://www.nokia.com/we-are-nokia/leadership-and-governance/group-leadership-team/justin-hotard/)).

The strategic signal of recruiting a data-center / AI operator rather than a telecoms lifer was unmistakable, and within seven months of his start Hotard had closed the NVIDIA equity-and-AI-RAN deal — a transaction that re-rated the equity ~4× and that arguably could not have been negotiated by a more traditional telco-equipment CEO ([Form 20-F p. 6 CEO interview](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/924613/000162828026015034/nok-20251231.htm)).

**Pekka Lundmark — outgoing CEO (2020–March 31 2025).** Lundmark stepped down on 31 March 2025 and continued as an advisor through end-2025. His tenure spanned the Russia exit, the Alcatel-Lucent post-merger residual integration, the smartphone licensing renewal cycle, and laying the groundwork for the Infinera acquisition ([Nokia leadership transition release Feb 2025](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2025/02/10/3023164/0/en/Inside-information-Nokia-announces-a-leadership-transition-Justin-Hotard-appointed-as-successor-to-Pekka-Lundmark.html)).

OnePlus & realme), vivo, Xiaomi, Honor, Lenovo, Mercedes-Benz, and the March 2025 Amazon settlement on video-streaming patents ([Form 20-F pp. 13, 24, 184](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/924613/000162828026015034/nok-20251231.htm)).

### Customers & customer-concentration disclosure

### Projection assumptions (FY2026E–FY2030E)

The base-case projections are anchored on CMD 2025 long-term targets (EUR 2.7–3.2 billion comparable operating profit by 2028, FCF conversion 65–75% of comp-OP) and the Q1 2026 raised NI growth guide (12–14% for full-year 2026, from the prior 6–8% CAGR framework).

**Network Infrastructure** is projected at +12% in FY2026 (mid-point of the raised 12–14% guide), tapering to +7% by FY2030 as the Infinera consolidation comp effect fades and the segment matures. Sub-segment trajectories: Optical Networks at +20% in FY2026 then tapering to +4% by FY2030; IP Networks at +11% in FY2026 on AI back-end / DCI wins, settling at +4%; Fixed Networks at low-single-digit growth throughout, reflecting xPON ASP refresh offsetting volume maturity ([Q1 2026 PDF — NI guide](https://www.nokia.com/system/files/2026-04/nokia_results_2026_q1.pdf)).

**Mobile Networks** is projected to decline at -1% per year on average — consistent with Dell'Oro's ~1% market growth forecast and ongoing AT&T share loss to Ericsson, partially offset by T-Mobile US wins ([Dell'Oro — RAN stabilized](https://www.delloro.com/news/ran-market-stabilized-in-2025/); [Nokia–T-Mobile US release](https://www.nokia.com/newsroom/nokia-lands-strategic-5g-ran-deal-with-t-mobile-us-to-enhance-nationwide-connectivity/)).

**Cloud and Network Services** is projected at +5–7% per year, driven by Private Wireless ramp at 20%+ CAGR offsetting flat-to-down legacy core/applications ([Form 20-F p. 22 — 5G SA share](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/924613/000162828026015034/nok-20251231.htm)).

**Nokia Technologies** is held flat-to-slightly-up at the EUR 1.4–1.5 billion run-rate; the 2024 catch-up year is non-recurring, but the contracted recurring revenue stream is locked through 2030+ ([Form 20-F p. 13](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/924613/000162828026015034/nok-20251231.htm)).

**Margin trajectory.** Comparable operating margin moves from FY2025's 10.1% to ~12% by FY2028 — within the CMD 2025 implied range. The bridge: NI mix shift (+100 bps), Optical margin ramp post-Infinera integration (+90 bps), Infinera-specific synergies (+60 bps), partially offset by RAN headwind (-80 bps) and modestly aided by corporate overhead cut from EUR 350 M to EUR 150 M (+10 bps net of reinvestment) ([CMD 2025 release](https://www.nokia.com/newsroom/nokia-announces-new-strategy-evolution-of-its-operating-model-new-long-term-financial-target-strategic-kpis-and-changes-to-its-group-leadership-team/); [Capacity Media — biggest overhaul in years](https://capacityglobal.com/news/nokia-announces-biggest-organisational-overhaul-in-years/)).

## Appendix Z — Deep-Dive Topics

### Z.1 The AI-RAN technology stack and why NVIDIA matters

The Nokia–NVIDIA partnership is the highest-leverage strategic bet on Nokia's books, and understanding the mechanics is critical for sizing the equity story. AI-RAN refers to the convergence of two adjacent compute workloads — the radio baseband signal processing that historically ran on purpose-built DSPs in Layer 1 of the RAN protocol stack, and AI/ML inference workloads (call quality optimization, anomaly detection, traffic-class prioritization) that operators want to run at the edge. NVIDIA's Aerial RAN Computer Pro (ARC-Pro) reorganizes both onto a single GPU-accelerated platform, theoretically delivering 5–10× compute efficiency vs purpose-built RAN hardware for combined workloads ([NVIDIA-Nokia release](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2025/NVIDIA-and-Nokia-to-Pioneer-the-AI-Platform-for-6G--Powering-Americas-Return-to-Telecommunications-Leadership/default.aspx); [Frank Rayal — NVIDIA/Nokia analysis](https://frankrayal.com/2025/11/03/nvidia-and-nokia-a-strategic-bet-on-edge-ai-ran/)).

Where Nokia adds value to NVIDIA's stack: the RAN protocol stack itself (3GPP Release 17/18 conformant Layer 1, Layer 2, and Layer 3 implementations honed over a decade of AirScale deployments), carrier-grade operational integration (NetGuard security, real-time orchestration via Cloud RAN, geo-redundant fault tolerance), and the global Tier-1 carrier relationships (T-Mobile US, SoftBank, KDDI, Indosat as named NVIDIA-partnership trial carriers in the 20-F p. 16) that provide the deployment launch pad. Without Nokia, NVIDIA has GPUs and software libraries but no path to a commercial 5G/6G product running in a real operator network. Without NVIDIA, Nokia continues to compete with its own ReefShark SoC against Ericsson's silicon — a multi-decade engineering treadmill with no obvious step-function payoff ([Form 20-F p. 16](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/924613/000162828026015034/nok-20251231.htm)).

The economics matter. AI-RAN's commercial GA target of late 2027 implies meaningful P&L contribution starting FY28 — perhaps EUR 200–400 M of revenue and EUR 30–60 M of operating profit in FY28 in our base case, scaling to EUR 800–1,200 M of revenue and EUR 150–300 M of operating profit by FY30. The Bull case assumes Nokia captures 5–10% of the cumulative USD 200 billion AI-RAN TAM that NVIDIA cited, generating EUR 1–2 billion of incremental annual revenue by FY30 with structurally higher margins than purpose-built RAN (likely 20%+ comp-OM given the software-heavy mix). The Bear case assumes the AI-RAN narrative is overhyped — that hyperscaler-grade AI integration at the radio edge has more friction than NVIDIA's marketing suggests, and that commercial GA slips to 2029 or beyond, capping benefit at trial-revenue only through the explicit forecast period ([NVIDIA investor release](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2025/NVIDIA-and-Nokia-to-Pioneer-the-AI-Platform-for-6G--Powering-Americas-Return-to-Telecommunications-Leadership/default.aspx)).

### Z.2 Optical-DSP roadmap consolidation post-Infinera

Optical Networks is where the Infinera deal is supposed to pay off. The strategic question for FY26–FY28 is how Nokia consolidates the two distinct DSP roadmaps: legacy Nokia's PSE-6s at 140 GBaud / 1.2 Tbps per wavelength on 5nm FinFET CMOS, vs Infinera's ICE7 at 148 GBaud / 1.2 Tbps per wavelength on a custom design optimized for the in-house Indium Phosphide PIC platform ([Nokia PSE-6s page](https://www.nokia.com/optical-networks/pse-6s/); [Nokia ICE7 page](https://www.nokia.com/optical-networks/ice7/)). The two products have overlapping addressable markets — both target hyperscaler DCI links at 800ZR/ZR+ pluggables and metro/long-haul transport at 1.2T/1.6T transponders — but distinct technical strengths: PSE-6s offers third-generation probabilistic constellation shaping (PCS) for capacity-distance optimization on legacy fiber plant; ICE7 offers vertical InP PIC integration for ~30% lower cost-per-bit and ~60% lower power-per-bit at peak baud rates.

The likely consolidation path, based on industry precedent (Cisco's Acacia integration, Marvell's Inphi integration), is multi-year. We model FY26 as the first year where Nokia ships unified product literature naming both DSPs under one optical brand; FY27 as the first year where field testing shows pseudo-comparable performance across both platforms; and FY28 as the first year where Nokia issues a forward roadmap converging to a single DSP family. Customer churn during this transition is the main execution risk — hyperscalers in particular dislike platform consolidations because their qualification cycles run 18–24 months and they prefer roadmap continuity over best-in-class performance. *Analyst view:* the EUR 833 M of goodwill and EUR 1,026 M of identifiable intangibles assigned to Infinera in the FY25 acquisition accounting will be tested on impairment by FY27 if the consolidation slips ([Form 20-F Note 6.2 p. 185](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/924613/000162828026015034/nok-20251231.htm)).

### Z.3 The Mobile Networks segment — what would change our view

Mobile Networks at 2.8% FY2025 op margin is the structural drag on the group story. Three pivots could change this. First, an AI-RAN inflection where ARC-Pro begins to actually capture incremental TAM in 2H 2027 — would lift MN margin into double digits by FY29. Second, an AT&T reversal or pause on the Ericsson Open RAN runoff — unlikely given the December 2023 contract structure but possible if AT&T's Open RAN deployments underperform technically ([AT&T release Dec 2023](https://about.att.com/story/2023/commercial-scale-open-radio-access-network.html)). Third, a new Tier-1 win in the EU (BT, Vodafone, Deutsche Telekom) or APAC (Reliance Jio, KDDI, NTT) that adds EUR 1–2 billion of annualized revenue at higher-than-segment-average margins ([Form 20-F p. 22 — 408 commercial 5G agreements](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/924613/000162828026015034/nok-20251231.htm)).

The 1 January 2026 reshape consolidates MN into the new "Mobile Infrastructure" segment together with microwave radio, FWA CPE, Site Implementation, and Managed Services. This is operationally clean — these are all carrier-facing low-growth-but-predictable businesses — but financially obscures the AI-RAN inflection when (if) it arrives. We model the new MI segment at flat-to-low-single-digit growth through FY30E with margin expanding from 3.5% (post-recast) to 6.0% by FY28 — well below the NI segment's projected 13–14% by then ([Recast segment results announcement](https://www.nokia.com/newsroom/nokia-provides-recast-comparative-segment-results-for-2025-and-2024-reflecting-new-operating-and-financial-reporting-structure/)).

### Z.4 Patent licensing — the second derivative

Nokia Technologies' EUR 1.4 billion contracted run-rate is the equity story's stabilizer. With Apple, Samsung, Huawei, OPPO, vivo, Xiaomi, and Honor all on multi-year licenses signed in 2023–24, the next major re-licensing cycle is FY28–FY30 — coinciding with industry transition to 6G. This represents both upside (6G-essential patents add to Nokia's portfolio depth; expansion programs in automotive, consumer electronics, IoT, and multimedia broaden the licensing base) and risk (handset OEMs are sophisticated counterparties who push back hard against royalty rate increases). The 20-F's disclosure of *"annual contracted recurring revenue of over EUR 800 million through 2030"* sets a hard floor that should hold even in a bear scenario where Nokia's expansion programs underperform ([Form 20-F p. 13](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/924613/000162828026015034/nok-20251231.htm)).

Nokia's video-codec licensing program is the most under-appreciated optionality in the model. Following the 2024 HP and 2025 Amazon deals, pending German enforcement against Acer, Asus, and Hisense could add incremental annual revenue if it settles favorably ([Form 20-F Note 6.1 p. 184](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/924613/000162828026015034/nok-20251231.htm); [Iclg.com — Nokia–Amazon settlement](https://iclg.com/news/22453-nokia-and-amazon-settle-international-patent-disputes/)). With the global streaming-device installed base measured in billions of units and per-unit royalty rates typically in the $0.10–$0.50 range, the video-codec program TAM is plausibly EUR 500–800 M of annual licensing revenue at maturity.

### Z.5 Quarterly trajectory expectations

Our base case implies the following quarterly cadence for FY2026 — useful for catalyst-mapping and earnings-day positioning. Q1 2026 actual: net sales EUR 4,500 M (+4% cc), comp-OP EUR 281 M (+54% YoY), NI +6% cc with Optical +20%, AI & Cloud customers +49% YoY and EUR 1 B of orders ([Q1 2026 PDF](https://www.nokia.com/system/files/2026-04/nokia_results_2026_q1.pdf)). For Q2 2026 (reporting July 2026), we model net sales of EUR 4,950 M (+4% YoY), comp-OP of EUR 460 M, with NI growth of +13% YoY consistent with the raised 12–14% full-year guide. Q3 2026 (October 2026) should show seasonal step-up in NI as hyperscaler ordering historically concentrates in 2H; we model net sales of EUR 5,100 M and comp-OP of EUR 550 M. Q4 2026 (January 2027) is typically the strongest quarter — net sales EUR 6,300 M, comp-OP EUR 800 M, delivering the full-year guide of EUR 20.9 B revenue and EUR 2.09 B comp-OP ([Q1 2026 PDF — guidance](https://www.nokia.com/system/files/2026-04/nokia_results_2026_q1.pdf)).

The single most important quarterly read is **Q3 2026** — by then, both the AT&T fiber deal scaling and Infinera integration synergies should be visible in segment-level margins; the hyperscaler order trajectory will be clear (does Q1's EUR 1 B repeat or accelerate?); and any NVIDIA AI-RAN trial color from T-Mobile US's Bellevue launch will have surfaced. A clean Q3 2026 print materially de-risks the Bull case and could trigger a model-driven re-rating; a soft Q3 (hyperscaler orders below EUR 700 M, Optical YoY growth below 10%) would put base case projections at risk ([Form 20-F p. 16 — T-Mobile US partnership](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/924613/000162828026015034/nok-20251231.htm)).

### Z.6 Capital structure and credit quality

Nokia's investment-grade credit profile (typically BBB/BBB+ across S&P/Moody's/Fitch) is supported by EUR 3.4 billion of net cash at FY25 end, EUR 4.9 billion of pension obligations that are partially externally funded, and IB liabilities of EUR 3.4 billion at a weighted average rate around 3.5% ([Form 20-F p. 66](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/924613/000162828026015034/nok-20251231.htm)). The debt maturity ladder is well-staggered with no single year exceeding EUR 800 M of obligation through 2030. Pension de-risking has been steady — Nokia has executed multiple buy-ins over the past five years reducing the gross deficit; we model EUR 500 M of remaining underfunding net of plan assets at FY25 end, with this declining steadily as obligations are settled.

The fortress balance sheet supports a self-funding capital return program. Cumulative FY2026E–FY2030E dividends of EUR 4.8 B and buybacks of EUR 5.2 B (total EUR 10 B of capital return) can be funded entirely from operating cash flow without leveraging up the balance sheet — leaving ~EUR 8 B of net cash at FY30E that could be deployed for further M&A or special dividends. *Analyst view:* the capital return story is structurally underrated by the market, which is focused on AI-RAN narrative; a 1% buyback authorization increase or a special dividend declaration would be a positive catalyst likely received above its EPS-arithmetic value ([Form 20-F p. 10 — capital return track record](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/924613/000162828026015034/nok-20251231.htm)).
