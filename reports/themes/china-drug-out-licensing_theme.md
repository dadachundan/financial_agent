# China Innovative Drug Out-Licensing / 创新黎明 2.0

**Created:** 2026-05-31 · **Last refreshed:** 2026-05-31 · **Last mutated:** 2026-05-31 · **Refresh cadence:** monthly · **Languages tracked:** en

## Thesis

China biopharma has crossed the inflection where ex-China rights to Chinese-originated drug assets are being purchased by global big pharma at deal values that materially reprice the originator equity. 2025 saw 157 out-licensing transactions worth a cumulative US$135.7 billion, versus 94 deals for US$51.9 billion in 2024 — a ~2.6× increase in deal count and a ~2.6× increase in headline biobucks year-over-year ([SCMP, 2026-02](https://www.scmp.com/business/china-business/article/3339011/chinese-drug-makers-strike-record-us136-billion-out-licensing-deals-2025); [Pharmasource, 2026](https://pharmasource.global/content/china-biopharma-out-licensing-surges-to-record-137-7b-in-2025-2026-on-pace-to-break-it-again/)). Q1–Q2 2026 has so far run hotter still, with average deal size up ~76% year-over-year on the same source basis.

The Morgan Stanley framing of "创新黎明 2.0 — from batch licensing to custom value capture" captures the structural shift: where the 2021–2024 wave saw Chinese biotechs bundle assets cheaply to monetise pipelines (Akeso/Summit $500M upfront for ivonescimab in December 2022 was the precedent), the 2025–2026 wave sees them negotiate selectively for nine-figure upfronts on a per-asset basis and double-digit royalties on commercialisation. Innovent's May 2026 Pfizer pact — $650M upfront, $9.85bn in development/regulatory/commercial milestones, double-digit royalties, and **co-commercialisation rights in the US and Europe** plus retained Greater China rights — is a structural milestone because of the co-commercialisation clause, not just the headline number ([Pfizer press release, 2026-05-29](https://www.pfizer.com/news/press-release/press-release-detail/pfizer-and-innovent-biologics-enter-global-strategic); [Innovent HKEX 1801 announcement, 2026-05-29](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2026/0529/2026052900029.pdf); [CNBC, 2026-05-29](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/29/innovent-shares-rise-10percent-after-pact-with-pfizer-of-up-to-10point5-billion.html)).

The bet behind this basket is that *the originator equity captures most of the deal upside*, not the buyer. A $15.2bn Bristol Myers Squibb / Hengrui multi-asset deal moves Hengrui (RMB 320bn market cap) far more than it moves BMS (US$140bn) ([BMS press release, 2026-05-12](https://news.bms.com/news/details/2026/Bristol-Myers-Squibb-and-Hengrui-Pharma-Announce-Strategic-Agreements-to-Advance-Innovative-Medicines-Across-Oncology-Hematology-and-Immunology-2026-EbQpaI6Zdc/default.aspx); [Hengrui Pharma press release, 2026-05-12](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/hengrui-pharma-and-bristol-myers-squibb-announce-strategic-agreements-to-advance-innovative-medicines-across-oncology-hematology-and-immunology-302769021.html)). The vulnerability is well-known: biobucks are mostly milestone-contingent, FDA AdComm and OS-readout risk is real (Summit's HARMONi-3 PFS interim missed its pre-specified threshold once before flipping favourable at the OS readout ([AllSci, 2025](https://allsci.com/news/clinical-trials/summits-ivonescimab-heads-toward-november-fda-decision-as-harmoni-3-interim-pfs-misses-threshold/))), and a single bilateral US-China policy event (the BIOSECURE Act-style escalation against WuXi names) can compress the CRO/CDMO leg of the basket faster than the innovator leg.

The basket separates *originators* (whose equity is repriced by deals) from *enablers* (CRO/CDMO that manufacture the assets globally) and *US partners* (where China-asset licensing is a material driver of equity, not background noise). Per-ticker performance dispersion is wide — RemeGen STAR +97% 1Y vs Zai Lab −44% 1Y on the same theme — so equal-weight basket reporting is for discipline only, not for portfolio sizing.

## Scope rules

**In:** Chinese-headquartered innovator biotechs that have closed (or are imminently likely to close) ex-China licensing deals with global big pharma where the deal value materially repositions the equity; CRO/CDMO houses whose manufacturing or development platforms enable the out-licensing wave; US-listed partner equities whose value is materially driven by Chinese-asset commercialisation (Summit is the cleanest current example). Both pure-play originators and diversified incumbents qualify if at least one closed major deal (>$1bn headline value, >$100M upfront) anchors the inclusion.

**Out:** US/EU buyers (Pfizer, Merck, BMS, GSK, AstraZeneca, JNJ) — too diversified for individual China deals to materially reprice the equity; pure generic / biosimilar players (Lonza-style cost arbitrage); medical device makers (separate sector); traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) names; private companies (tracked in the watch list, not the basket). Contract-research-only houses without proprietary innovator IP are also out — Tigermed is in because it has a global clinical-trial platform that enables Chinese-asset INDs in the US and EU, not because of any standalone IP.
